# Ants · pass 03 · subject notes

Five new directions. None of them draws an ant body; each one draws what the colony does or leaves behind. None goes back to pass 01 (orrery, elevation, survey, field-and-figure, formicarium) or pass 02 (glockenspiel, ant cutaway, leaf, twig fork, 3D ants). All five are rest frames at the defaults (ants 4, scent 50, fade 30, scale pent), 480 × 220, with the stock header and no text below it, in grey only. They come from one generator, `sketch.py`. v2 to v5 are modelled in world units and projected through one camera each. Lint is 0 errors and 0 warnings on all five, with no `text-in-drawing`. `design_pass_submit` returned `ready_for_pick`: every pair differs in angle and language, and subject overlap is at most 0.09. I judged each one at 1:1 from `vN/rest.png`.

**v1 · mechanism · hatched mass.** A hand-held marble board. Its rocker gates are pushed toward whichever side the marbles take, and springs pull them back. The route the gates are thrown onto is one bright groove, with marbles in it.
Strong: it explains the rule in one picture (deposit, fade, choice) and it's crisp, with a clear brightest line. Weak: it reads as a Galton board (chance) first, it shows the preferred outlet rather than the phrase, and the board's hatching is a lot of lines.

**v2 · material, close · edge-lit silhouette.** Sand grains seen close and low, each lit along its upper edge. A swept channel bends past the pebbles (the degrees), and scent beads lie on its floor, shrinking back to dried marks.
Strong: it's the only one where scent is a substance you can see, and the lit beads are pretty. Weak: at 1:1 the grain field is busy and can read as gravel or a starfield, the beads are small, and hundreds of grains are far over budget.

**v3 · one scene · detailed line art.** A ford of stepping stones, one row per step and one stone per degree. The route everyone takes is worn bare and dished, the rest is mossed over (hatched), and one stone, where the front ant is, is brightest.
Strong: it's the most physical and the most of a place. "Used stays bare, unused grows over" reads without any explanation, and water ripples and stone flanks make it feel authored. Weak: it's the busiest frame and the most expensive, the hatched tops can look like biscuits, and the crossing's order is implied rather than drawn.

**v4 · one detail, large · hidden-line 3D.** One iron ring at large scale, with ropes running off the frame to the rest of the lattice. The way the colony uses is laid up thick and taut (the brightest element). The others hang slack and have frayed through, hatched past the break.
Strong: the calmest and blackest frame, with one obvious subject and the frame edge doing real work. Weak: it reads as a mooring before it reads as a colony, it shows one junction rather than the phrase, and the thin ropes and their ghosts are close to the 1:1 limit.

**v5 · the trail itself · bold closed outlines.** The trail as an object: a band swept clear through litter in one closed loop past the stones it plays, with hatched old spurs fading out and seeds carried round the loop.
Strong: it's the most direct answer to "draw the trail", it shows the phrase as a loop that has set, and the outlines are bold and legible. Weak: an oblique closed band can read as a race track or a pool, it sits near pass 01's loop idea, and the twigs and spur ends are the weakest drawing.

## Ranking (my view as the designer; the pick is the owner's)

1. v3: the strongest picture that only this machine could have. A path worn by use and overgrown by neglect is the whole rule, and it's a real place. It needs thinning for budget and calm.
2. v1: the clearest mechanism, and the rule is literally its moving parts. It needs the phrase brought back in (for example, the outlets lighting in order).
3. v5: the most literal trail, clean and bold. The risk is the track or pool reading.
4. v4: calm and well built, but the metaphor needs explaining.
5. v2: the scent shown as material is the right idea, but the frame is too busy at 1:1.
