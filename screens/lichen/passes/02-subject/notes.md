# lichen · subject pass 02 · notes

Five new directions after pass 01 was turned down (no orbital rings, no labelled growth charts). Rest frames at defaults (grow 35, decay 20, seed 1, maj), all from `sketch.py` and the same colony model at tick 14 (14 living, 7 dead, 2 born, one spore due). 3D variants (v1, v2, v5) are modelled in world units and projected through one camera each. No text below the header; states are line weight, brightness, movement or 45° hatching. Lint: 0 errors and 0 `text-in-drawing` on all five; the remaining warnings (v2 4, v4 2, v5 4) are `legibility` on parts under 3 px (hatch ends, the plate's ridge tops, spore dashes), to be cleaned up at frames. Judged at 1:1 (`vN/rest.png`).

## v1 · cutaway section · the rule as a physical thing
What it is: a stone block cut open along the start row, the crust on it in section (thickness = age, cortex line, rhizines into the stone) with the rest of the colony as rounded areolae behind; grow pushes tongues, decay lifts hatched flakes, seed drops spores, half-step joints are cracks in the stone.
At 1:1: the strongest object, calm, and every knob is something you could do to real stone and crust; weak in that the section shows only one row, the tongues and flakes are small at defaults, and the areola plates edge towards toro-blocks' cubes in silhouette.

## v2 · exploded view · a cutaway of the object at work
What it is: the colony taken apart into a floating stack: lattice plate with raised half-step ridges, hatched dead sheet, living sheet with holes where it died, this tick's births lifted out on dashed assembly axes, spores on top.
At 1:1: the clearest account of the rule (lattice, dead, living, new, spores are separate and stacked in order); weak because the 72-tile plate takes the lower half and most of the line for a part only scale moves, and a hole's far wall reads as a stray cylinder.

## v3 · single-weight contour · the trace left behind
What it is: only the trail of births since the first spore, one weight throughout: worn through (dashed) where cells died, jogged on half steps, the two births of this tick bright with a ring.
At 1:1: the most unusual and the most black, and scale shows as a gait in the line; weak because at rest it's a sparse scribble that says neither lichen nor lattice, and the jogs are small.

## v4 · hatched mass · one detail at large scale
What it is: a window on the margin at large scale, the lattice stretched by the scale (half steps are narrow cells), cracked black areolae, the dead centre as hatched mass, the new cell bright, lobes pushing into bare stone.
At 1:1: big, legible parts and the hatching works; weak because it fills the frame (the busiest, 84 % black), loses where the colony is overall, and the stretched lattice can read as random paving.

## v5 · edge-lit silhouette · the lichen itself
What it is: a foliose thallus lying on stone, black, lit along edges facing the light, lobes reaching out of the living margin, hatched crumbling centre, fruiting cups on half-step cells, born lobes the brightest rim.
At 1:1: the most "lichen" and the calmest silhouette, and the edge light gives the OP-XY's one-bright-thing look naturally; weak in that the lobes still read a little like a fringe, the unlit side vanishes, and it's the closest in idea to pass 01's v4 (a literal patch), though drawn quite differently.

## Ranking (designer's, not a pick)

1. v1: one physical object where each knob does something real, in a drawing language that sits next to theseus-tower.
2. v5: the machine's own name drawn well; needs better lobes, but the edge light suits the device.
3. v2: the clearest explanation of the rule; needs the plate made quieter.
4. v4: legible detail but busy, and loses the whole colony.
5. v3: distinctive, but too sparse to read at rest.
