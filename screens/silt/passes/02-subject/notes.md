# Silt · pass 02 (subject) · notes

All five come from `sketch.py`. Each is modelled in 3D and projected through one camera, drawn at the defaults (settle 1.0 s, layers 16, stir 35, grain 0) and judged at 1:1 from `rest.png`. Each lints to 0 errors and 0 warnings, with no `text-in-drawing`. The body is 89–95 % black, and `#CDCDCD` is used once per frame. Uniqueness: `ready_for_pick`, every pair differs in angle and language, and subject word overlap is at most 0.12. Nothing from pass 01 comes back: no strata with labels, no plans, no elevations, no scope.

**v1 · exploded view · the material, seen close.** One hexagonal mica-like flake per partial, exploded up a dashed leader line. The big fundamental sits on the bed and the small high partials are lifted furthest, with the top flake lit.
Strong: the exploded-view convention *is* the stir (the gaps grow with partial number), and the growth steps on the upper flakes give real line-art detail. Weak at 1:1: it reads a little like a chain of hexagons, the low flakes crowd into a muddy cluster at the base, and settle-as-tilt needs pre-drawn variants for every flake.

**v2 · single-weight contour · one scene.** A pebble has just dropped into a still stream. A plume of billows rises off the bed under rings on the water and leans downstream.
Strong: it's the most immediate "a note stirs silt" picture, and it'll animate naturally (the plume billows up, leans, sinks). Weak at 1:1: at rest the plume can read as a shell or a cabbage. With one weight the hierarchy rests on brightness alone, and the bed and far-bank lines read as plain stripes.

**v3 · hatched mass · the rule as a real object.** A snow globe of silt: a hatched drift on the floor, and a hatched cloud lifted off it whose top edge is the bright line.
Strong: every knob maps to something anyone knows from the toy (shake harder, thicker liquid, more flakes, coarser flakes). The hatching does exactly what the craft rule asks (semi-opaque cloud vs denser solid drift), and it's calm and centred. Weak: the toy could tip into novelty, layers-as-hatch-count saturates near 40 lines, and the gap between the cloud and the drift is easy to misread.

**v4 · edge-lit silhouette · cutaway at work.** A prospector's pan cut in half mid-swirl. The tilted waterline is lit; the fine grains ride up the high side while the heavy ones stay in the crease.
Strong: it shows the DSP's rule exactly (light/high partials lift furthest, heavy ones stay), and stir as a big rotation of one bright line is the clearest control channel in the pass. Weak at 1:1: the pan reads as a bowl or boat as much as a gold pan, the grains are near the 3 px floor, and the right side is busy with hatching and back-wall hairlines.

**v5 · detailed line art · the trace left behind.** A slab of rippled silt after the water has gone. The crests sit on the top, the cross-laminae show in the cut face, and the front ripple profile is the bright line.
Strong: it's the most finished-looking and the most "OP-XY": a modelled object with hidden-line edges, inner structure and one lit profile, calm and full-width, and nobody else's machine would draw it. Weak: settle, stir and grain all reshape the same ripple field (asymmetry, height, crest planform), so their channels are a family rather than distinct, and it reads as "ripple marks" before "partials".

## Ranking

1. v5: the strongest drawing at 1:1, clearly this machine's subject, and it has room for detail because the subject stays on screen. It needs one control moved off shape (settle could become the laminae count or the slab's tilt).
2. v4: the most faithful to the rule, and stir has the best channel. It needs a clearer pan silhouette and less hatching on the right.
3. v3: the most legible metaphor for a player, and a clean use of hatching. The risk is novelty.
4. v2: the most alive once it moves, but the weakest still.
5. v1: an honest exploded view, but at 1:1 it reads as a hexagon chain.

The pick is the owner's.
