# Hinge · subject pass 01 · notes

Five sketches at 1:1 (`v1..v5/rest.png`, rendered with `rsvg-convert -w 480 -h 220 -b black`), all generated by `sketch.py` at the defaults (hold 200 ms, creak 0, swing 3.2 s, mix 50), all lint 0 errors 0 warnings. Uniqueness: `ready_for_pick`; every pair differs in pattern and language, subject overlap ≤ 0.07.

**v1 · P4 Stack in depth · survey plan.** The doorway in plan once per swing, the plans stacked up the hinge pin: the newest swing bright at the front, older and wider swings as grey plans behind.
Strong: the stack says "back and forth, slowing" in one look, and the pin as the stack's axis is a good find. Weak at 1:1: the black sectors chop the back plans' dashed arcs into fragments, the plan oblique is not really a survey plan, and swing and mix both change the stack's silhouette.

**v2 · P10 Voice field · patent section.** FIG. 1, a section through a louvred leaf between hatched stiles: each slat turns to the caught slice's level, and the rows below are later passes, played reversed and quieter.
Strong: the model is the drawing (slice across, passes down, ping-pong as alternating rows), every control has its own geometric job, and it looks like a patent figure. Weak: rows of short slats come close to tally marks or hatching at 1:1, the third row is faint, the door itself is only implied by the knuckle and stiles, and a hold or creak turn re-poses all 66 slats in one frame.

**v3 · P15 Cell field · hidden-line isometric.** An isometric floor of cells with the door's swing swept into it as a fan of raised cells, the rim lit.
Strong: crisp, big, cheap to read as "something lit on a floor", and the hidden-line slabs are clean. Weak: at 1:1 the fan reads as an L-shaped wall of blocks, not a swing, and P15 is meant for sounds without a mechanism, which hinge isn't; hold and swing both just change how many cells light.

**v4 · P1 Section drawing · star atlas.** The hinge cut in section at the pole of a graduated half-planisphere, its leaf on the meridian, and the swing plotted as an ephemeris spiralling in to rest, turning points as stars sized by loudness.
Strong: the most authored frame: one bright leaf, lots of black, labels that belong to the language, and the decaying swing reads as plotted motion. Weak: it can read as a protractor or gauge, the hinge itself is small, the inner turns crowd near the pole at high swing, and the track needs two or three mutable outlines.

**v5 · P11 Instrument stack · engineering schematic (stretch).** A door in elevation as a schematic: three knuckled hinges on a dash-dot axis, a closer and arm on the head, a plank leaf ajar, callouts and a dimension.
Strong: unmistakably a door, and each part owns one control in a way a player would guess (closer = swing, hinges = creak, ajar = mix). Weak: it only half fits P11 (nothing is selected or struck; it is really P1 in schematic dress), it shows the door but not the swinging, and the hinges and closer are small next to the leaf.

## Ranking

1. v4: best looking at 1:1 and the only one that draws the swing itself coming to rest; controls map to separate channels.
2. v5: clearest object and most guessable mapping, but a weak stretch and a static picture of a moving thing.
3. v2: most honest model of the DSP, held back by tally-mark slats and an implied door.
4. v1: the right idea (swings stacked on the pin) in a drawing that clips itself into fragments.
5. v3: crisp but doesn't read as a door, and the wrong pattern for a sound with a mechanism.

The pick is the owner's.
