# Kiln · pass 02 · subject notes

Five rest frames at the defaults (strike 50, glaze 30, crack 0, fire 1.12), 480 × 220, stock header, no text below it, from `sketch.py`. Bowls, mallets and ring courses are modelled in 3D and projected through one camera per frame. Lint: 0 errors, 0 warnings on all five. Judged at 1:1 from `vN/rest.png`. The body is 89–95 % black in every frame, and each has one #CDCDCD element.

**v1 · mechanism · hidden-line 3D.** A yarn-wound mallet poised over a thrown bowl. Strike sets the backswing, glaze the hatched interior line, crack a crack down the wall, fire the stiffened profile.
Strong: it's plainly a struck bowl at a glance, the hidden-line rings hold at 1:1, and the mallet head is the one bright thing. Weak: it's the most expected picture. Backswing reads as force, but strike is hardness. Fire's profile change is small.

**v2 · material close · bold closed outlines.** An edge-on fragment of the rim wall in close-up, with pores in the body, a hatched glass skin and a felt tip on the lip. Strike sets the tip size, glaze the skin thickness, crack a fissure, fire the number of pores (they close up).
Strong: the most original idea, and fire as vitrification is physically right. Bold outlines read well. Weak: the slab can read as a generic curved bar rather than a bowl's rim. The left fracture is fussy. Pores counting down can pop.

**v3 · scene · cutaway section.** The kiln opened in section, with brick walls, a sprung arch, one bowl on the shelf, a bent pyrometric cone beside it and a tapping rod through the spy hole. The cone's bend is fire, the rod's stand-off is strike, the hatched outside band is glaze, and a dunting crack is crack.
Strong: the only frame where the machine's name is the picture. Fire gets a real potter's gauge (the cone), and the bowl stays the bright subject inside quiet brickwork. Weak: the bowl is small (~125 px) and the cone ~35 px, so crack and fire need checking at 1:1. The frame is the busiest of the five.

**v4 · machine's rule · exploded view.** The bowl taken apart along its axis into four ring courses and a foot, spaced like its modes, with the mallet lifted off along its line of strike. Fire stretches the upper gaps, crack splits every course into halves, glaze climbs hatched course by course.
Strong: it maps the DSP's rules most directly (stretch, doublets) without drawing a diagram. Weak: at 1:1 the stack reads more as a lampshade or spring than a bowl. The lower courses crowd, and it's the smallest drawing on screen.

**v5 · cutaway at work · single-weight contour.** The bowl cut through its axis, all in one line weight, with the bright cut wall, the interior's height contours, a hatched glaze pool and the mallet just off the lip.
Strong: big, calm and handsome, and the contours give the interior real depth. Glaze as a rising pool reads instantly. Weak: with one weight there's no Subject stroke, so only brightness separates the cut face. Fire's wall-thinning travel is small. It's the closest in spirit to pass 01's section, though it's a 3D cutaway with no chart.

## Ranking (reviewer's view; the pick is the owner's)

1. v3: the subject comes from what the machine is called and does, and every control is a physical thing in the scene.
2. v1: the clearest struck bowl, and the mallet is the mechanism. Safe but strong.
3. v5: the best-looking single drawing; it needs a way to carry fire.
4. v2: the boldest idea; the fragment has to read as a rim.
5. v4: the right rule but the wrong read at 1:1.
