# Marble · subject pass 01 · notes

All five sketches come from `sketch.py`, drawn from one model at the defaults (6 rows → 7 bins, 1/8, bounce 0, major around c). Each lints at 0 errors and 0 warnings and was judged at 1:1 from `vN/rest.png` (`rsvg-convert -w 480 -h 220 -b black`). Uniqueness: `ready_for_pick` (every pair differs in pattern and language; subject overlap at most 0.13).

**v1 · P14 loom · engineering schematic.** The last four bars as cloth: seven warp threads (bins, placed by pitch), four weft rows (bars), every landed marble a slab unit on its thread, with dimension and leader annotations.
Strong: history without a list (you see the bell build across bars), and the schematic text makes it look authored. Weak: eight drops over seven bins make the front row sparse low boxes, so at 1:1 one row doesn't read as a bell; the cloth looks scattered rather than woven.

**v2 · P5 field and figure · oscilloscope trace.** A scope graticule as the field, the odds of each bin as one bright stepped trace of pulses at their pitches, the bar's drops as a strip below.
Strong: the clearest at 1:1; the bell, the root and the scale's gaps read in a glance. Weak: the most generic picture of the five (any spectrum analyser looks like this), and the board itself never appears; b and c pulses 16 px apart nearly merge.

**v3 · P12 orrery · typeset diagram.** A radial board as an orrery: hopper as sun, six peg-row orbits, bins as planets round the pitch circle sized by odds, one marble's spiral out to c, a book-figure caption.
Strong: the most surprising; pitch as a circle is true to pitch classes, and the waiting marbles round the sun are a calm drop clock. Weak: two subjects (board and pitch circle) compete, the edge bins are 3 px planets at the back, and the orbits don't turn for any reason.

**v4 · P13 capture · orthographic elevation.** The board in front elevation: hopper and gate, a triangle of pegs, bins cut as pipes so each floor sits at its pitch, the captured pile drawn bright.
Strong: the machine's own shape, nothing to learn; the pile is the distribution, and pitched floors are a pun that's also exact. Weak: the pile bunches into a bright clump at 1:1 and bounce has no part of its own (a drafting dimension is bolted on); scale only moves floors 4–8 px.

**v5 · P3 trace as subject (stretch) · survey plan.** One marble's fall as a survey traverse from the hopper station through columns of peg stations to a lot on a pitch-spaced boundary, with the bar as a chain line.
Strong: the bright traverse is a clean single subject with a lot of black, and the survey furniture keeps it from being a scope. Weak: it shows one outcome, not the odds, which is what the machine is about; the triangle stations edge toward icons. The stretch doesn't really fit: a trace explains a waveform, and this machine's subject is chance.

## Ranking (designer's, not the pick)

1. v4: the machine's own shape and the strongest P13 fit; needs a better home for bounce and more travel for scale.
2. v2: clearest at 1:1, every control has its own part; risks looking generic.
3. v1: the only one that shows history; needs denser cloth (fewer bins shown or taller units) before it reads.
4. v3: the most original but splits the eye between the board and the pitch circle.
5. v5: a good drawing of the wrong thing; the stretch doesn't fit.
