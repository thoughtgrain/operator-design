# Relay · subject pass 02 · notes

Five new directions after pass 01 was turned down (scope sweeps, bar spiral, cell wall, well long-section, tipping buckets: none of those objects or looks comes back). All five come from `sketch.py`, one script, each 3D thing modelled in world units and projected through one camera per variant. They're drawn at the defaults (stages 1000 / 100 ms, leak 0, clock 0, feedback 35 %) and judged as `rest.png` at 1:1. `lint_frame.py`: 0 errors, 0 warnings on all five, so no `text-in-drawing`. Body black share is 87–94 %. Grey only, one `#CDCDCD` element each, three weights (1 / 1.5 / 2 px). Every control was also rendered at both extremes to confirm it can be drawn; nothing in `definition.json` changed. Submitted: `ready_for_pick`, all ten pairs differ in angle and language, subject overlap ≤ 0.10.

**v1 · material, seen close · exploded view.** The held charge as a row of thin capacitor plates pulled apart along a dash-dot axis out of a hatched source block, plate height = bias + note, the note and two smaller recirculations riding down the row.
Strong: it reads as an exploded assembly with real depth, and the repeats are visible as groups. Weak: at 1:1 the plates read as dominoes or glass panes; stages and clock are both position along the row, and the bright peak plate is small.

**v2 · one scene · single-weight contour.** A telegraph line running to the horizon, the poles the stages, the note a lit span of the top wire and its recirculations dimmer spans further down.
Strong: the calmest and most atmospheric frame, with real perspective and lots of black, and leak (a wire hanging loose) and clock (leaning poles) are physical. Weak: the pulse is a small 2 px span, it's a metaphor for relaying rather than this chip, and far poles crowd at 20 stages.

**v3 · the rule on a real object · hatched mass.** A long cradle of hanging steel balls: the output ball swung out bright, the input ball swinging back by feedback, shadow sides hatched.
Strong: it reads instantly at 1:1, and every control has its own channel (count, shape, position, rotation). The returning swing is feedback without a legend, and hatched mass suits it. Weak: a Newton's cradle is a familiar desk toy and can read as "momentum" rather than "delay", and strings tangle when clock is high.

**v4 · cutaway at work · edge-lit silhouette.** A DIP-8 package with its moulding cut back, hatched section faces, the die on its lead frame with bond wires, and the chain as a meander across the silicon carrying a lit note and a return trace.
Strong: the most specific to a bucket-brigade chip. The cutaway explains it without labels, and the rim-lit edges look authored. Weak: it explains the device more than the sound, the meander every control acts on is only about a quarter of the frame, and the dark `#323232` outlines may vanish on the device.

**v5 · the trace left behind · detailed line art.** Ring stains on a plank floor where each bucket stood, later passes set down over the first, offset and broken, with the freshest ring bright.
Strong: the most original idea and specific to a bucket brigade. Feedback as rings over rings and leak as drip trails need no explanation. Weak: the floor fills the frame and is the busiest ground of the five, the rings with tide-marks edge toward coffee-cup or target icons, and at 15 rings with high feedback they crowd into a chain-link pattern.

## Ranking (mine; the pick is the owner's)

1. v3: the clearest physical reading at 1:1, with four distinct channels and feedback as a returning swing
2. v5: the most original and the most this-machine idea; it needs a quieter floor
3. v4: the most specific object, but it explains the chip more than the sound, and its subject area is small
4. v2: the best atmosphere, but the subject (the pulse) is too small and it's a metaphor
5. v1: sound idea, but at 1:1 it reads as dominoes and two controls share position
