# ebb · subject pass 01 · notes

Five sketches at defaults (tide 12 st, period 8.0 s, foam 0 %, scale maj, not muted, at rest), generated by `sketch.py`, each linted to 0 errors and looked at as a 480 × 220 PNG at 1:1. The parts are static art; budgets are argued in each `candidate.json`. The pick is the owner's.

**v1 · P13 Capture · orthographic elevation.** A stepped sea wall with one tread per scale degree; the water is the capture line and the highest tread it covers (outlined bright) is the note.
Strong: the rule is the picture (height is register, the steps are the scale, hw/lw are the tide), it reads at once at 1:1, and every control has its own part. Weak: at 1:1 it's close to a bar chart, the 22 joint lines are busy, and the bright block is only 17 px wide.

**v2 · P14 Loom · survey plan.** Four rows of the last 16 s woven as weft threads over sixteen chainage lines, slabs on the threads, newest row bright at the front, with a scale bar in the corner.
Strong: history is visible, and with foam the solid/hollow roles show what changed pass to pass. Weak: at defaults the rows are near-flat dashes in a ruled box and read like a meter grid; the plan view loses the loom's depth, and the scale bar is a legend.

**v3 · P5 Field and figure · patent section.** Fig. 1: a float cut and hatched, riding a receding sea, its graduated spar passing a fixed index arm on a pile, the stops on the pile showing the tide's range, recent notes ticked in a strip.
Strong: it looks the most authored of the five (numerals, section hatch, one bright hull), and the receding sea gives period a large, readable field to reshape. Weak: there are many parts to learn (pile, collars, index, spar), the numerals are decoration, and the 3 px semitone ticks sit on the floor.

**v4 · P12 Orrery · hidden-line isometric.** The moon on its orbit drawing the sea into a bright spheroid round a dashed, hidden earth, with pitch classes as beads on a ring.
Strong: it's the true cause of a tide, Kepler gives period an honest channel (orbit radius), and the spheroid is a strong single bright shape. Weak: concentric ellipses edge toward the target-ring look, the scale is fill state on small beads (close to shade-only), and the register (the actual note) isn't readable anywhere.

**v5 · P10 Voice field · star atlas (stretch).** A star chart of the last 16 s: each degree at each moment is a star sized by how near the tide passes, so the notes trace an ecliptic toward a bright "now" star.
Strong: crisp and clearly labelled, and tide, period and scale all reshape one field. Weak: at 1:1 it's a scatter plot of pitch over time (a piano roll in star dots), not an atlas, and it scrolls the whole field on every column. Honestly it doesn't fit: the exception argument holds on paper, not on the screen.

## Ranking

1. v1, the clearest mapping of rule to picture
2. v3, the most authored drawing
3. v4, the best metaphor with the weakest scale and register reading
4. v2, a dashboard risk at defaults
5. v5, a stretch that doesn't fit
