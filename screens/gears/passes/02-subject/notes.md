# Gears · subject pass 02 · notes

Focus from the owner: more literal, the gears themselves, meshing toothed wheels as the subject, no keyboard, no labels. Sketches: `vN/rest.svg` (+ `rest.png`, rsvg-convert at 480 × 220 on black), all from `sketch.py`, defaults (3:2, 8 teeth, mesh 100, maj), no notes. The 3D scenes (v1, v2, v4, v5) are modelled in world units and drawn through one fitted camera each; v3 is flat and face-on. Every frame lints at 0 errors with no `text-in-drawing`; the remaining warnings are near-reversals at ribbon ends and a few sub-3 px hatch or pin fragments. Nothing below the header is text. Body black: 88–96 %.

**v1 · cutaway section.** A gearbox with its front wall and lid cut away and the cut faces hatched; A and B meshed on their shafts, B carrying an index plate pinned at the scale's degrees.
Strong: it reads at once as a machine with its insides explained, and the casing frames the pair. Weak at 1:1: the busiest of the five; the casing and plate compete with the meshing tooth, and the index pins are at the 7 px floor.

**v2 · exploded view.** B lifted up its axis off arbor and washer, both faces scored with one groove per mesh at the note's radius.
Strong: the only one that keeps the music on the gears (grooves are the 24-step cycle, scale moves them radially), and the exploded lift reads as an exploded view. Weak at 1:1: A's grooves are small and compressed by the camera; the meshing is implied by a dashed line, not shown.

**v3 · single-weight contour.** The pitch point at large scale: A rising from the bottom edge, B coming down from beyond the top, B's tooth in A's gap, A's inner rim notched at the scale's degrees.
Strong: calmest and cleanest at 1:1, real involute teeth, the most OP-XY, and lint is spotless. Weak at 1:1: B is only an arc, so ratio reads from curvature alone; one weight leaves the contact tooth bright only by colour.

**v4 · hatched mass.** Two thick spur wheels on a low camera, shaded flanks hatched, B's web drilled at the scale's degrees.
Strong: the most literal and solid; hatched flanks give real mass without grey fills, and mesh as teeth stepping down is a clear physical change. Weak at 1:1: closest to a generic 'gears' illustration, and hatch on narrow flanks would be costly to re-cut as the wheels turn.

**v5 · edge-lit silhouette.** The hand-cranked pair close up, black masses lit on the upper-left edges, the crank handle brightest, B's spokes lit at the scale's degrees.
Strong: the crank makes it a mechanism in the hand, and the drawing is the most atmospheric. Weak at 1:1: the shadow side of everything nearly vanishes, and a spoke per semitone is a convention, not mechanics; spokes lit by angle and by scale membership fight as B turns.

## Ranking (designer's view; the pick is the owner's)

1. v3: calmest, most precise, the meshing itself is the subject; each control changes the drawing at the size it's drawn.
2. v4: the literal object drawn well, solid without grey fills; mesh has the clearest physical channel.
3. v1: the cutaway explains the mechanism but needs thinning (drop the bosses or the inset panel) to let the mesh lead.
4. v2: the best musical idea (grooves as the record of the cycle), weakest gear read at 1:1.
5. v5: handsome but the scale channel is invented and the shadow side loses half the drawing.
