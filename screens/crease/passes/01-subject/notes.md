# Crease · subject pass 01 · notes

All five rest sketches at defaults (crease 2.00, sym 0, paper 40, mix 100), generated by `sketch.py` from one model of the folder, lint 0 errors (v5: 4 warning lines covering ~350 2 px dither rects, by design of P9), judged at 1:1 from `vN/rest.png`. `design_pass_submit('crease')` → `ready_for_pick`, no problems.

**v1 · P4 stack in depth · patent section.** The strip folded into plies, seen obliquely as a stack of sheets; creases join them at alternate ends, the folded wave is inked on the top ply, with reference numerals 10/12/14 and "fig. 1".
Strong: the most literal picture of the machine's name, crease as a count of plies is honest (one more ply per fold), and it's calm with lots of black. Weak: at ×2 it's three flat slabs and a thin wave, the creases (the subject's best idea) are small 1.5 px bends at the ends, and the opaque plies hide their own back creases; crease steps a ply at a time.

**v2 · P10 voice field · hidden-line isometric.** Slabs whose heights are the fold surface (input swept across, level rising to the back), creases lit as the brightest tops.
Strong: the model is the drawing, so all four controls reshape it truthfully and there's no metaphor to explain. Weak: at 1:1 it reads as a lumpy heap and as toro's sibling, not a new screen; the fold isn't legible as a fold, and 210 nodes for a picture that doesn't say "paper".

**v3 · P15 cell field · star atlas.** The harmonics as a band of 20 px cells about an atlas plate's equator, odd columns only at sym 0.
Strong: the crispest at 1:1, and sym as stripes filling in is the single clearest picture of what symmetry does to a folder's sound. Weak: it is a spectrum analyser in a frame, a dashboard of meters; the atlas is only the border and ticks, and a folder has a mechanism, so Gate 0 argues against texture.

**v4 · P1 section drawing · engineering schematic.** A folding press in section: the web runs in, festooned over a fixed upper bank of rollers and under a lower bank that drives down; infeed nip, mix diverter blade, depth dimension.
Strong: one bright subject (the web) with grey structure, every control owns a physical part on its own channel (bank position, bank shift, roller scale, blade rotation), and it looks authored at 1:1. Weak: it's a machine that folds paper, not the signal folded; the loop count is fixed, so crease deepens but never adds folds, and the mix blade is small.

**v5 · P9 one-bit ink · oscilloscope trace (stretch).** A paper streamer folded into the output wave, front face light dither, back face dense, hard white outlines, on scope corner brackets with a trace-position marker for sym.
Strong: the strongest single image at 1:1, and the only one where the drawing is the output wave and also reads as paper; two faces of the paper is a real reason for dither. Weak: on the device the moving panels can't be dithered (P9's own budget rule), so it would ship as flat greys and lose its point; panels overlap in time order, not depth order, and it's outside the category's column.

## Ranking

1. v4: the most finished-looking and the cleanest channel map; the risk is that it pictures a folder, not the fold.
2. v5: the best picture of the sound itself; only works if the owner accepts flat-grey panels on the device, which is a different screen from this sketch.
3. v1: the right idea (plies are folds) drawn too small at the default; push the creases and the stack could overtake v4.
4. v2: truthful but illegible as folding, and too close to toro.
5. v3: a meter; keep its sym-as-stripes idea for whichever subject wins.

The pick is the owner's.
