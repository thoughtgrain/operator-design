# Ringer · subject pass 02 · notes

Five new directions after pass 01 was turned down ("Five new, unique iterations"). One script (`sketch.py`), all at defaults (6 bells, plain bob, 2 a beat, stroke 0 %), showing the real plain bob minor change 426153, so bell 4 is in first place and is the lit part in every frame. No text below the header in any frame; every `rest.svg` lints at 0 errors and 0 `text-in-drawing` (v4 carries warnings for its small hatch pieces at 2.6–2.9 px). Body black: v1 90 %, v2 94 %, v3 83 %, v4 86 %, v5 94 %. Judged at 1:1 from `vN/rest.png`.

- **v1 · sallies at large scale · detailed line art.** The striped grips at eye height, ropes cut off by the top and bottom edges; each sally's height is its bell's place, the lowest being pulled now.
  Strong: the calmest and most specific to the machine; a ringer knows it at once, and the wave of heights *is* the change. Weak: method, pace and stroke all move sallies vertically (order, spread, one held up); a non-ringer may see capsules.
- **v2 · the bells in striking order · hidden-line 3D.** Hidden-line bronze bells hung along one frame side, standing in the order they strike, nearest first.
  Strong: the literal object drawn properly from one camera, quiet, one bright bell. Weak: bells sliding along a beam isn't how a tower works; the back of a twelve-bell row shrinks under 10 px.
- **v3 · wheels and ropes · bold closed outlines.** The wheels face on, ropes wound from the garter hole, each turned to how far through its pull its bell is.
  Strong: the mechanism really is the rule, in-plane rotation is cheap, bold and legible. Weak: the busiest frame; at twelve it gets small; echoes pass 01 v2's idea (turned to the swing) in a new drawing; the sliders are small.
- **v4 · the ring cut through · cutaway section.** Bells in a two-tier frame sectioned on their centre plane, clappers standing off the sound bow by how far each is from striking.
  Strong: the wall thickness and the strike point are things only this machine would show. Weak: three controls on the clappers' angle; hatched cut bronze conflicts with hatching meaning "inactive"; can read as a rack of bell icons.
- **v5 · the tower pulled apart · exploded view.** The bell floor lifted off the ringing floor, ropes between, a bright line threading the rope feet in striking order.
  Strong: the only one that shows the whole tower and the order at once; stroke as a literal gap. Weak: small in the frame; the threading line is a diagram on the scene and leans toward a sequencer's star.

## Ranking (the designer's; the pick is the owner's)

1. **v1**: the most this-machine and the calmest at 1:1; the change reads as a wave of sallies with no diagram. Its channels need separating (method re-deals, pace scales).
2. **v3**: the mechanism with every control physical, cheap on the device; needs thinning to survive twelve bells.
3. **v2**: the bells drawn well, but the sliding row is a stage, not a tower.
4. **v5**: the best idea of the order, the weakest drawing at 1:1.
5. **v4**: authored-looking cut, but three controls share one channel and the hatch says the wrong thing.
