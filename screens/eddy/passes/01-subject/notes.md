# eddy · subject pass 01 · notes

Five sketches of the rest frame (defaults: spin 0.82 Hz, flutter 0, depth 50 %, mix 100 %), all from `sketch.py`, linted at 0 errors and looked at 1:1 (`vN/rest.png`). The pass is `ready_for_pick`; the pick is the owner's.

**v1 · P1 section / oscilloscope trace.** A plan section of the horn chamber: the twin horn is a single bright flared outline about the hub, on a dim scope graticule, with a fading phosphor trail behind each mouth and two faint ghost horns.
Strong: one obvious object, calm, lots of black, and the rotor reads as spinning without moving. Weak at 1:1: the ghost horns (1 px, #4B4B4B–#646464) nearly disappear, and the graticule ticks edge toward instrument-panel decoration.

**v2 · P2 diagram + trace / typeset diagram.** Left half a labelled signal path (in, rotor wheel with speed ticks, eddy line feeding it, dry/wet vane); right half a bright tone the doppler squeezes over its grey dry copy.
Strong: the screen and the hands agree (spin and flutter left, depth and mix right), and the trace says what the sound does. Weak at 1:1: six labels and a switch-like vane read as a panel; the two traces cross often and tangle; spin as a tick count is a convention, not a picture.

**v3 · P3 trace as subject / orthographic elevation.** The horn mouth's path through time as a helix in side elevation, front strokes bright, back strokes as hidden lines, projected from an end view of the rotor at the left.
Strong: the most literal model (radius is depth, pitch is spin), and mix as a dry-to-wet crossfade along time is clear. Weak at 1:1: it reads first as a sine wave with alternate strokes greyed, which is the "it's just an oscilloscope" pitfall; the helix only shows if you look for it.

**v4 · P4 stack in depth / survey plan.** Five survey sheets of the rotor receding up-right, one per past revolution, the horn's bearing moving on from sheet to sheet; the front sheet carries bearing ticks and the horn's throw lobe as contours, with a scale bar and bearing label.
Strong: unusual and authored-looking; spin as the bearing fan and flutter as sheets slipping out of register are both readable. Weak at 1:1: the lobe on the front sheet is small for the brightest element, mix as 2–5 sheets is under the count floor, and "past revolutions" is a fiction on a page with no time input.

**v5 · P12 orrery (stretch) / patent section.** The cabinet cut open: one shaft carrying the horn and drum rotor on two projected orbits, phantom horn positions trailing the bright mouth, louvres in the walls, hatched base, numerals 10–16 on leaders.
Strong: a rotary speaker really is a two-body orrery, so the stretch is honest; the patent language with numerals makes it look drawn rather than generated. Weak at 1:1: the cabinet box and hatch frame the body like a panel, the louvres read as zigzags, the leader to 10 runs long, and an effect page never orbits on its own, so the orrery's main virtue (calm constant motion) is gone.

## Ranking

1. **v1**: the clearest single object; every control has a part of the horn to move and the frame is mostly black.
2. **v5**: the strongest idea (the mechanism is literally an orrery), held back by the boxed cabinet and a pattern built for motion the page doesn't get.
3. **v4**: the most original picture; needs mix moved off sheet count and a bigger front lobe.
4. **v2**: honest and buildable, but closest to a dashboard.
5. **v3**: the cleanest model, but at 1:1 it looks like a sine wave.
