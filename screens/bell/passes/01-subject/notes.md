# Bell · subject pass 01 · notes

All five rest sketches at defaults (depth 12, air 0.2 s, knee 6.0, gain ×2.00), from `sketch.py`, lint 0 errors / 0 warnings, judged at 1:1 from `vN/rest.png`. Every trace is the compressor's model on one fixed four-hit passage: an effect page doesn't get the audio.

**v1 · P2 diagram + trace · patent section.** Fig. 1 the bell in section on its cable under the surface, waterline inside, reference numerals 10 and 12; fig. 2 the output pressed down inside its grey input, threshold dashed.
Strong: the only variant where the bell and what it does to the signal sit side by side; the patent section suits a bell, and depth/air/knee each own a physical part. Weak: two half pictures, and the bright trace outshines the bell, so the metaphor is secondary in its own screen; gain lives only in fig. 2.

**v2 · P3 trace as subject · hidden-line isometric.** The passage's envelope extruded into a wall, standing on a makeup plinth, pressed down through a water plane, the input's grey wall behind it.
Strong: the most honest picture of compression in the set (you see the crests pressed below their grey originals), and the language sits next to toro-blocks. Weak: at 1:1 the wall is only ~50 px tall to keep the plane out of the header, so the crests read small; there's no bell, only water; depth and air both reshape the front face.

**v3 · P4 stack in depth · star atlas.** Water strata as seven stacked chart plates every 8 dB; the bell plotted as a body between 8 and 16 with an air-pocket phase and an ephemeris track down from the surface.
Strong: calm, lots of black, one clear brightest element, and the labelled plates make depth legible as depth. Weak: it draws the settings, not the sound; the half-lit disc is one step from a level-indicator icon; the plate tick borders get close to hatching.

**v4 · P10 voice field · engineering schematic.** 22 air columns, one per input level, as tall as the bell lets that level out, so the crowns are the transfer curve; pressed crowns bright, a dashed second crown for the release, datum and knee band dimensioned.
Strong: the transfer curve built from parts, which explains threshold, knee and makeup at a glance. Weak: at 1:1 it reads as a bar chart, the dashboard the protocol warns about; air's dashed crowns are barely visible inside 11 px columns.

**v5 · P6 figures · oscilloscope trace (stretch).** A stick diver in one beam, crouched under the bell on channel 2, air escaping as dots, on a dashed graticule.
Strong: it gives the screen a human scale, and the bell itself reads well as a scope trace. Weak: clip art at 1:1, the P6 caution exactly; knee as a crouch is a pun, not a mapping. A compressor has no performer, so it doesn't fit.

## Ranking

1. v1: keeps the bell as the subject and explains what it does; push fig. 1 to carry more weight (bigger bell, gain on the cable or surface) so the trace doesn't win.
2. v2: the truest image of compression; needs more height and a bell of some kind or it's a scope with a camera.
3. v3: the most beautiful and the calmest, but a diagram of the knobs.
4. v4: clearest explanation of the curve, but too close to a meter.
5. v5: doesn't fit; keep only as a mascot if the owner wants one.

The pick is the owner's.
