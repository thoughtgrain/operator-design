# Ferry v5 · cleanup

Generated by `clean.py` (model and helpers imported from `../sketch.py`, which I didn't touch). Defaults: route up, 4 ports, swell 0, dwell 1/8. Lint: 0 errors, 0 warnings. Not picked yet, so it stays in the pass folder.

- I kept the idea: survey sheets of each port stacked in route order, with the front sheet the harbour where the ferry lies and its berth as long as the dwell.
- I removed every label. The port names on the sheet edges, the front sheet's name, and "1/4" are gone. Each sheet now says which port it is by where it sits: one step back per call in route order, and lifted 1.6 px per semitone above the front port. That puts the route's melodic contour into the stack's skyline (up climbs, and the octave port jumps highest; down would fall away behind). Ports is still the number of sheets.
- I simplified the front sheet to one harbour: a quarter-ellipse coast (Line weight, `#969696`), two depth contours 8 px out (hairline, `#646464`), and one straight pier square to the coast. The survey border ticks are gone, because at 1:1 they crowded the frame and read as a ruler.
- I moved the berth into open water. It's now a dashed box across the pier head, clear of the contours, instead of a box crossing them. Its length is still the dwell (30 + 24 × dwell px).
- I made the ferry read as a ferry. It's a 34 × 10 px double-ended hull (both ends the same blunt point, as a real ferry is) with its wheelhouse cut out in black amidships. It's solid `#CDCDCD`, the one bright element. Swell turns it slightly off the berth's line, and it wobbles the contours.
- The back sheets are plain outlines over black, stepping down from `#7D7D7D` to `#646464`. They aren't filled, and position tells them apart, not shade. The stack is centred in the body both ways, nothing clips, and the body is about 95 % black.
- One thing still to judge: back sheets are blank, so a sheet farther back doesn't show its own harbour. That keeps the frame calm, but it means pitch shows only as height, not as each port's own drawing.
