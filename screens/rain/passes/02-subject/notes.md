# Rain · subject pass 02 · notes

Five new directions after pass 01 was turned down (no roof plans or sections with numbers, no pulse trains, no labels). All five come from `sketch.py`, drawn at the defaults (rate 1.0 a beat, roof 12 st, gust 0, puddle 0), every 3D part modelled and projected through one camera per frame. Each frame lints at 0 errors and 0 warnings, so there are no `text-in-drawing` warnings. The only text is the stock header. Each was judged at 1:1 from `vN/rest.png`. Uniqueness: `ready_for_pick`, every pair differs in angle and language, and the highest subject overlap is 0.13.

**v1 · the verge, edge-lit (edge-lit silhouette).** The roof's gable end seen side on: a black mass whose rounded tile noses climb off the top-right corner, lit only on their sky-facing edges. Rain streaks hang in the open sky above it, and a half-round gutter sits at the eave.
Strong: it's the calmest and blackest of the five, and the rim-light idea gives roof a clean channel (noses inside the span are lit, the ones past it are dim). Weak: the scale isn't really "large" yet, since the roof is a thin climbing edge in the middle. The barge-board hairline reads as a stray diagonal, and the gutter (puddle) is a small corner.

**v2 · the drops (detailed line art).** The last bar's raindrops falling through still air, each drawn as a real drop (round on top, flat underneath when big) with a hairline strobe ghost above it. Size is pitch, and the newest drop is bright.
Strong: it's the most literal, and the strobe ghosts make "falling" read without speed lines. Size-as-pitch is physical and roof gets a clear range. Weak: at 1:1 the drops can still read as bubbles. Nothing at rest shows puddle, and the node cost grows fast with rate.

**v3 · the rainstick (hidden-line 3D).** A rainstick across the screen: a hollow tube with a cord grip, growth nodes, an open mouth and a spiral of pin heads. Each ring of pins is a course, and struck pins carry rings (the newest is filled bright).
Strong: it's the best "thing in the hand" and the most surprising idea. Every control has a physical part (pin rings, struck heads, tipping the stick, pebbles in the mouth) and it can't be another machine's screen. Weak: unstruck pin heads are 4 px dots at the size floor. The stick looks tilted even though its tip angle is 0 (that's the camera's yaw), and puddle is invisible at rest.

**v4 · pantiles, close (bold closed outlines).** Eight courses of S-shaped clay pantiles rising away from the viewer. Each lapped edge is a bold line over black, the rolls are carried up as hairlines, and there are teardrops and a crown on the struck tile.
Strong: it reads instantly as a wet tiled roof, the struck tile's bright edge is a strong note response, and hatched water in the pans is a natural puddle. Weak: it's the busiest and least black frame (~81 % black), the far courses crowd toward the header, and at 36 st it will turn into a hatch.

**v5 · flowerpots under the eave, cut away (cutaway section).** A shelf of clay flowerpots graded big to small, each cut through to show its walls, rim collar and drain hole. Drips fall from the eave's tile noses above, and the pot that just rang is bright.
Strong: it's a charming, legible scene, and the cutaways explain where the water goes (puddle as hatched water in each pot is very clear). Weak: at 1:1 the row of graded vessels is close to a bar chart, there's a lot of empty band between the eave and the pots, and the eave wave can read as a divider.

## Ranking (designer's, not the pick)

1. v3: truest to "only this machine", with a physical part for every control and a strong single subject. It needs bigger pin heads and a level rest angle.
2. v4: reads best at 1:1 as rain on a roof, with a strong note response and a natural puddle channel. It needs calming and more black.
3. v2: the literal drops with strobe ghosts are honest and calm. Puddle and node cost are its problems.
4. v1: the calmest frame and a nice lit/unlit roof channel, but not yet large or authored enough.
5. v5: a good scene, but it sits closest to a chart of bars at 1:1.
