# Spool · subject pass 01 · notes

Five rest sketches from `sketch.py`, all at defaults (wear 0, wow 0, length 0.32 s, bias 0), linted to 0 errors and 0 warnings, judged at 1:1. Uniqueness: `design_pass_submit` → `ready_for_pick`, every pair differs in pattern and language, subject overlap ≤ 0.09. A check at wear 0.7 / wow 0.6 / length 0.8 / bias 0.2 (scratch copy, not kept) confirmed every control moves its part.

**v1 · P2 diagram + trace · typeset diagram.** A labelled tape path (loop, capstan, tension roller, rec and play heads, wow arm) on the left, a rule from the play head to the cycle drawn large on the right.
Strong: explains the machine without a legend, and the trace keeps crisp corners at rest. Weak at 1:1: the left diagram is small and thin beside the trace, the controls interleave left/right against the encoder order, and the wow arm's swing lifts it off the tape.

**v2 · P3 trace as subject · orthographic elevation.** The written cycle repeated bright along a strip of tape seen flat, cut by two diagonal splices, with a dimension line for the loop.
Strong: the boldest and most immediate at 1:1, one bright subject filling the body, and each control has its own part (trace, tape edges, splice, rails). Weak: it's one step from an oscilloscope; the rails sit close to the tape edges and read as a double line; four repeats cost all four paths.

**v3 · P4 stack in depth · survey plan.** Five passes as profiles receding diagonally, now in front, chainage ticks and pass numbers along the edge.
Strong: the only engine-column direction that shows the thing spool is about, wear pass by pass, and hidden-line fills keep the stack clean. Weak: at defaults the five profiles are identical, so the rest frame is a stack of copies; wow shear and bias scale are under their floors as sketched.

**v4 · P5 field and figure · patent section.** The record head's core, hatched in section with coil turns and reference numerals, standing on a perspective plane of tape, with a strip of 24 loop segments.
Strong: the most authored-looking of the five; it sits naturally beside the field-and-figure reference and the stock engines' "one object" look. Weak: the head is the brightest thing and no control moves it; wear (score 5) lands on the strip, which is empty at rest; wow's edge weave is far under the shape floor.

**v5 · P14 loom · hidden-line isometric (stretch).** Each pass a row of isometric slabs on warp threads, heights tracing the cycle; the front row solid, older rows in hidden-line wire.
Strong: the stretch has a real case, since spool's sound is a history of passes and the loom draws sameness and drift pass to pass. Weak: at rest the rows are identical and it reads as a 3D bar chart (a meter); ~168 nodes, all re-keyed every pass; slabs get small at 24 threads.

## Ranking (mine; the pick is the owner's)

1. **v2**: reads instantly at 1:1, every control has a part, cheapest to make work.
2. **v4**: best drawing language, but needs wear moved onto something prominent.
3. **v3**: closest to the idea of a wearing loop; dull at rest unless wear defaults above 0.
4. **v1**: clear but weak as a picture; the left half needs to grow.
5. **v5**: the stretch is arguable but costs too much and drifts toward a meter.
