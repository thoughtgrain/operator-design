# Rain · subject pass 01 · notes

All five sketches come from `sketch.py`, drawn at the defaults (rate 1.0 a beat, roof 12 st, gust 0, puddle 0), each linted at 0 errors and judged at 1:1 from `vN/rest.png` (`rsvg-convert -w 480 -h 220 -b black`). Uniqueness: `ready_for_pick` (every pair differs in pattern and language; subject overlap at most 0.14). Lint warnings left: hairline (1 px) rings and meridians in v1/v4, 1.8 px cluster dots in v4, and v5's 2 × 2 dither cells; all are sketch-stage and named in the candidates' risks.

**v1 · P12 orrery · survey plan.** A round pavilion roof from straight above: eight course rings round a finial, four drops on their courses (newest bright), a double gutter ring, north point, scale bar, labels.
Strong: calm, lots of black, the courses-as-notes reading is immediate and puddle-as-orbit is the one honest cycle. Weak: at 1:1 it's concentric rings with a dot in the middle, close to the target shape craft.md bans; gust and puddle are invisible at rest, and rain isn't a cycle.

**v2 · P13 capture · patent section.** A mono-pitch roof cut in section: lapped tile plates on a hatched rafter, two hatched posts, a half-round gutter, vertical streaks, the struck course bright, callouts 10–16 and "fig. 1".
Strong: the most authored-looking of the five at 1:1; one clear brightest element where the drop landed, and roof as pitch angle is a big, physical control. Weak: there's no pitch axis, so "which note" is implied; the gutter (puddle's whole effect) is a 20 px corner far from the eye; streaks are thin and sparse.

**v3 · P14 loom · hidden-line isometric.** The roof plane in isometric: eight tile columns as warp threads, four rows (now, −1, −2, −3) climbing to the ridge, struck tiles raised as slabs, this bar solid, older bars wire.
Strong: the best fit to the rule: rows show the rain's history, puddle gets a perfect channel (repeats as solid columns down the cloth) and gust shears rows into a diagonal. Weak: at 1:1 the slab grid reads close to an egg crate or step matrix; the roof slope is too gentle to say "roof" yet, and the labels sit loose.

**v4 · P5 field and figure · star atlas.** A conic graticule in the lowest grey, the roof as a gable constellation of course stars (atlas-broken lines, magnitudes, "tegula"), four meteor trails pointing back at a radiant, a dotted open cluster for the puddle, a 16-beat strip.
Strong: every control has its own place even at rest (radiant = gust, ridge star = roof, trails = rate, cluster = puddle), the brightest star is the note that just played, and labels make it look authored. Weak: the gable of dots is the house outline in disguise; trails pointing up at their source invert how rain falls; small stars and cluster dots are at the size floor.

**v5 · P9 one-bit ink (stretch) · engineering schematic.** A gable roof with alternating dithered course bands under a 2.5 px bright outline, a dithered wall, flat rain streaks, a wind glyph and a puddle plane, wired to labelled terminals r1, f1, p1, g1.
Strong: the one-bit printing is crisp at 1:1 and the dither bands make the courses legible. Weak: it doesn't fit, plainly: it's a house icon with rain, showing the setting rather than the rule; the terminals are a legend panel; roof (the most visible control) would have to redraw dithered still art.

## Ranking (designer's, not the pick)

1. v4: reads best at 1:1, every control already has a distinct part and channel, nearest the stock calm.
2. v3: the truest picture of a player (history, repeats, drift), but needs a steeper roof and fewer, larger slabs so it stops reading as a grid.
3. v2: the best-drawn single frame, weakest as a rule picture; puddle is too small.
4. v1: calm and correct but a target at 1:1; half its controls are empty at rest.
5. v5: the stretch doesn't fit.
