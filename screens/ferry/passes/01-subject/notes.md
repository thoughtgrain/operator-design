**v1 · P5 Field and figure · engineering schematic.** A ferry in side elevation (centreline, waterline, hidden hull line dashed) on a receding sea mesh, with a timing diagram of the timetable along the bottom.
Strong: reads as "ferry" instantly and the schematic language is consistent; the pulse staircase is a clear picture of route × ports. Weak at 1:1: the bright ferry only answers swell, and three of four controls are squeezed into a 28 px strip.

**v2 · P12 Orrery · oscilloscope trace.** An X-Y graticule with an orbit per octave, ports placed by pitch class, the route lit along the orbits, the beam spot as the ferry.
Strong: dwell as a burnt-in beam spot is a good native image, and the octave orbits make ports-adding-octaves legible. Weak at 1:1: the graticule dominates and carries no data, the triad clusters on one side of each orbit, and the "ferry between ports" story is mostly gone.

**v3 · P13 Capture · typeset diagram.** A bay in plan: piers spaced by interval along the shore, a dashed capture ring round each pier head, numbered legs of the route arcing across the water, the ferry docked in the first ring.
Strong: every control has its own part of the one picture (legs, piers, roughness, rings), the labels make it look authored, and the long return leg tells you the route at a glance. Weak at 1:1: rings collide at 8 ports with long dwell, the pier names and leg numbers need up to 9 text slots, and the bottom third is empty at defaults.

**v4 · P14 Loom · orthographic elevation.** The timetable woven as cloth in flat elevation, one band per voyage, slabs raised to pitch on warp threads, newest band part-woven at the foot.
Strong: swell gets the most honest picture of the five (straight columns break into slipped slabs), and dwell as thread count is plain to see. Weak at 1:1: at swell 0 it is four copies of one skyline, there is no ferry in it at all, and short dwells blow the node budget.

**v5 · P4 Stack in depth · survey plan (stretch).** Survey sheets of each port's harbour stacked diagonally in route order, the front sheet's berth drawn as long as the dwell.
Strong: the stack is a literal picture of route × ports and looks calm and printed. Weak at 1:1: route rides on text labels, the front sheet is the same generic harbour for every port, and each note swaps the whole front sheet; honestly, it mostly doesn't fit a player.
