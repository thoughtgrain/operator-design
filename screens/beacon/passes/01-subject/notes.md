# Beacon · subject pass 01 · notes

All five sketches come from `sketch.py`, drawn at the defaults (pulse 25, lens 83, sweep 2.4 st, fog 0), each linted at 0 errors and 0 warnings, and judged at 1:1 from `vN/rest.png`. Uniqueness: `ready_for_pick` (every pair differs in pattern and language; subject overlap at most 0.10).

**v1 · P3 trace as subject · survey plan.** The voice's output traced as a bright line along a chained baseline. Each input pulse below the chain rings into a run of teeth one comb period apart; station a's bearing ray is the light.
Strong: it reads at once at 1:1, and the model is honest (teeth spacing = pitch, run length = lens, pulse width shows in the grey input). Weak: without the chain, figures and offsets it's an oscilloscope. Fog isn't on screen at its default 0. The bearing ray is a thin mark in a corner.

**v2 · P4 stack in depth · patent section.** Sawtooth Fresnel plates in section recede behind a hatched lamp housing with a shutter blade, numbered 10–16 like a patent figure.
Strong: "comb lens" taken literally (a Fresnel profile is a comb), and plate count is a clean lens control. Weak: at 1:1 the offset plates read as stairs or a book's edge. The lamp is small and far from the bright plate, so the eye splits. It sits close to the stock prism engine's lamp-and-optics idea.

**v3 · P5 field and figure · hidden-line isometric.** A ringed lens barrel stands on an isometric sea. Its lit window and the light patch on the water turn with sweep, and the pulses tick along the strip.
Strong: one bright figure on a low-grey ground with lots of black, so it's the calmest of the five and nearest the stock look. Weak: a ringed cylinder is a generic primitive that says "lighthouse" more than "comb filter". The lit window at the barrel's edge nearly disappears, and the sea patch reads as a loose shard.

**v4 · P10 voice field · star atlas.** The spectrum as a star field on a conic chart. Columns are harmonics, rows are trips round the comb, the fundamental is the brightest star, and a constellation line runs down its column.
Strong: the drawing comes from the same model as the sound, and the pulse width's spectral null already shows as a lane of faint and hollow stars. The graticule and labels make it look authored, and it's the most novel of the five. Weak: the star field is close to a dot grid, which can read as a meter. "Rotating light" ends up as an abstract line across the chart. Pulse and lens both act on star size, so the perceptibility check will have to separate them.

**v5 · P14 loom (stretch) · engineering schematic.** A block schematic of the comb loop (sum, z-T, g, fog) with its delay line woven below as four rows of slabs on twelve warp threads.
Strong: the comb really is an echo field, which P14 already names, and the schematic labels make the mechanism explicit. Weak: the loom is history over musical time, and these trips last milliseconds, so it doesn't really fit. Two pictures share the screen, and the schematic half is a dashboard no control moves. The cloth is small and crowded at 1:1.

## Ranking (reviewer's, not the pick)

1. v1: clearest at 1:1, honest mapping, each control has its own part.
2. v4: most original and model-true; needs work so the field doesn't read as a dot grid.
3. v3: calm and stock-like, but its subject is the most generic.
4. v2: the strongest pun, but at 1:1 it reads as stairs.
5. v5: the stretch doesn't fit; the cloth shows physics, not history.
