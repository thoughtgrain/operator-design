# drill · pass 02 · subject · notes

The owner's focus: true to the name, an old crank hand drill. All five are that drill (a brace or an eggbeater drill), each from its own angle. Rest frames at the defaults (ratio 2.00, teeth 6, wear 0 %, bite 35 %), from `sketch.py`, every 3D part modelled and projected through one camera per frame, judged at 1:1. The stock header is the template's, unchanged; no text below it. All five lint at 0 errors and 0 `text-in-drawing`; the only warnings left are `legibility` on small ribbon pieces (v1: tiny runs of the curl near the hole). Uniqueness: every pair differs in angle and language, subject overlap ≤ 0.25; state `ready_for_pick`.

**v1 · the mark it leaves · single-weight contour.** The shaving: one long helical curl of wood rising out of the bored hole in a plank, its outer face bright and its inner runs mid grey.
At 1:1: the most unexpected picture and unmistakably drilling, with lots of black (96 %). But all four controls reshape the same strip, and the cropped plank reads a little like a ramp.

**v2 · one detail, huge · hatched mass.** The eggbeater's face wheel cropped by the left edge: hatched cast web, a rim of solid lugs, the bright pinion on the lugs, the spindle running off the right edge into a chuck.
At 1:1: the strongest graphic and the clearest mechanism-to-sound link (lugs = teeth, pinion travel = bite, wheel size = ratio). But it's the densest frame (82 % black), and using hatching for a solid's mass stretches craft.md's meaning of hatching.

**v3 · the object · edge-lit silhouette.** A carpenter's brace lying level, black silhouettes rim-lit along their top edges, its auger bit sunk into a board shown in section.
At 1:1: reads instantly as "a hand drill", calm, very OP-XY in its restraint. But the brace is thin (7 px rod) and small in a wide frame, and the teeth control lives on the bit's small flute grooves.

**v4 · the mechanism in the hand · detailed line art.** The eggbeater drill in three-quarter view: crank and turned grip on the face wheel, lugs driving a bright pinion, from the wooden head handle to the chuck and bit.
At 1:1: the most complete and literal drill, and every part is a real part. But it's the busiest: the hub, crank arm and spindle all cross in the middle, and bite as the bit's length is the weakest link to the sound.

**v5 · the material, close · hidden-line 3D.** A plywood block cut away through a bored hole: plies stacked up its faces, the score marks on the far wall, the cone at the floor, and the cut edge of the bore as the one bright line.
At 1:1: handsome and solid, and bite as hole depth is a big, honest move. But the drill itself never appears, and the score lines (ratio) are faint hairlines that may not clear the floor.

## Ranking (mine; the pick is the owner's)

1. v2: one big, calm, physical subject where every control is a part of the gear train doing what the DSP does; needs a lighter hatch or less web.
2. v3: the drill named, drawn literally, quiet and legible; needs a bigger brace or a tighter crop to use the frame.
3. v4: the full mechanism and the richest line art; needs the centre untangled and a better bite channel.
4. v1: the most original idea, true to drilling; four shape controls on one strip are the risk.
5. v5: a good drawing of the material, but the drill is only implied.
