# Tilt · subject pass 01 · notes

All five generated by `sketch.py` from one model of the effect at its defaults (focus 1k, tilt 0, blur 40 %, mix 100 %) applied to one fixed test chart, linted (0 errors, 0 warnings each) and viewed at 1:1 (`vN/rest.png`). Uniqueness: `design_pass_submit` → `ready_for_pick`, every pair differs in pattern and language, subject overlap ≤ 0.17.

**v1 · P3 trace · typeset diagram.** One bright spectrum line on a labelled log-frequency axis (`100`, `1k`, `10k`, `dB`, `Hz`), jagged in the focal band, a smooth hump outside it, with a dark dry ghost underneath.
Strong: reads in a second as "sharp here, soft there"; the axis labels make it look authored, and tilt as a literal rotation about the focus is the most honest mapping in the pass. Weak at 1:1: the ghost's peaks under the line add clutter at mix 100, and it's one step from "analyser", so the fixed test chart has to be obvious.

**v2 · P4 stack · orthographic elevation.** Six octave cards receding up-right, the 1k card crisp with a bright 2 px outline and a detailed wave top, the others with flattened tops and a lifted hairline echo.
Strong: depth means focus without a legend, and the bright card is clearly the one brightest thing. Weak at 1:1: the echoes of the front cards cross the cards behind and make a nest of wavy lines on the left; the front card is a big empty box; focus can only step by octave cards.

**v3 · P10 voice field · survey plan.** A neat-lined plan of 24 × 8 square markers (frequency by time) with a surveyed strip at the focus where markers are solid and bright on the three hits; outside, the hits are smeared into even hollow markers.
Strong: the contrast between the crisp strip and the even haze is exactly what blur does, and the strip lines read as a survey boundary. Weak at 1:1: it reads as a grid of LEDs or graph paper, close to a dashboard; tilt and blur both resize markers (needs blur moved to stretching markers into streaks); a turn redraws far more than ~20 nodes.

**v4 · P15 cell field · patent section.** A block of 22 px cells, solid bright cell pairs on the hits inside the band, 45° section hatching in long vertical runs outside.
Strong: the strongest texture contrast of the five: crisp short marks in the band against smeared cut material reads as tilt-shift at a glance, and at 40 % black it holds the body well. Weak at 1:1: the hatching can read as "disabled" stipple; hatched vs not can't be a palette swap, so it costs two nodes a cell (~320) and sdk 1.8; P15's noise-only rule has to be argued.

**v5 · P13 capture · hidden-line isometric (stretch).** A row of 22 fins on a model base in a 24°/30° dimetric view, an upright glass plane of focus across it; fins inside its depth of field are solid, the rest hollow with a ghost set back behind them.
Strong: the most literal tilt-shift picture (a miniature with a plane of focus), and the solid fins at the plane are the clear subject. Weak at 1:1: the row reads as a bookshelf or dominoes first; ghosts are only a few px at blur 40 %; tilt and mix have no twin in the optics (mix is a ruler on the base edge), so the stretch half fits.

## Ranking (mine; the pick is the owner's)

1. v1: clearest at 1:1, every control has its own channel, cheapest to build.
2. v4: strongest picture of what the effect does to texture; the cost and the hatching read are the open questions.
3. v2: depth-as-focus is right, but the echoes need taming.
4. v5: best story, weakest fit; half its controls are scenery.
5. v3: right idea, wrong look; it's the closest to a dashboard and has a channel clash.
