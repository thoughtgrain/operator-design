# drill · pass 01 · subject · notes

Sketches are rest frames at the defaults (ratio 2.00, teeth 6, wear 0 %, bite 35 %), from `sketch.py`, judged at 1:1. All five lint at 0 errors, 0 warnings. Uniqueness: every pair differs in pattern and language (subject overlap ≤ 0.16); state `ready_for_pick`.

**v1 · P1 section drawing · typeset diagram.** The wheel as a textbook figure: a big driver rim (ratio × the wheel) from the left, a follower lever biting the teeth on the right, a/b/c leaders and a caption.
Strong: one bright, chunky wheel that reads instantly as a gear, the mechanism is literally there, and bite as a lever swing is a clear rotation. Weak at 1:1: the driver's partial rim reads as a bracket, and wear has to live on the follower tip because teeth owns the wheel.

**v2 · P2 diagram + trace · orthographic elevation.** Gear pair with centre lines, pitch circle and datum on the left, the bent windowed wave on the right with one tick per tooth passed.
Strong: says both how (gears) and what (wave), and the split matches the encoders (structure on 1–2, tone on 3–4). Weak at 1:1: the left is busy grey linework and the 35 % bend is mild, so the two halves compete; wear and bite share the trace.

**v3 · P3 trace as subject · survey plan.** The output wave as a proposed alignment bent off the dashed existing one (the plain sine), with station ticks per tooth and chainage per wheel turn.
Strong: the cleanest screen of the five, lots of black, and "proposed vs existing" is an honest picture of what bite does. Weak at 1:1: no gear anywhere, so the machine's identity rests on survey dressing; close to the "oscilloscope" pitfall, and the dashed line is nearly invisible under the bright one.

**v4 · P4 stack in depth · patent section.** The rim unrolled into a hatched rack, one strip per wheel turn, stacked receding up-right, with a pawl biting the front strip and patent numerals.
Strong: the most distinctive picture, and the stagger between turns is a true and new way to show a non-integer ratio. Weak at 1:1: five strips of teeth start to read as hatching, and at the default whole ratio the stack is perfectly aligned, so the loudest control is invisible at rest.

**v5 · P12 orrery · hidden-line isometric (stretch).** An epicyclic train: sun driver on its arbor, a carrier arm, one bright planet wheel inside a toothed ring.
Strong: handsome and calm, mechanically true (an orrery is a gear train), and hidden-line keeps the overlaps clean. Weak at 1:1: the pattern's point (a population of items in a cycle) is absent, the ring is a lot of grey teeth for one control, and it plainly doesn't fit the category beyond the gear pun.

## Ranking (mine; the pick is the owner's)

1. v1: the subject is the mechanism, every control moves a part of it, and it's closest to the stock "one object" look.
2. v4: the most original idea, with a stagger that shows ratio in a way no other variant can; needs ratio given a second cue at whole values.
3. v2: sound and safe; earns its split, but the left half needs simplifying.
4. v3: clean but generic; the gear is missing.
5. v5: looks good, doesn't fit.
