# Silt · pass 01 (subject) · notes

All five drawn by `sketch.py` at the defaults (settle 1.0 s, layers 16, stir 35, grain 0), linted at 0 errors, judged at 1:1 from `rest.png`. Uniqueness: `ready_for_pick`, every pair differs in pattern and language, subject overlap ≤ 0.11.

**v1 · P2 Diagram + trace · oscilloscope trace.** A scope with three graticules: the partial spectrum and a dB decay line (settle as rotation) on the left, the stirred output waveform on the right.
Strong: every control has an honest, physically correct channel, and the columns line up (settle/layers left, stir/grain right). Weak at 1:1: three boxed graticules read as a dashboard of meters, and grain's sideways slide is a few px, well under the position floor.

**v2 · P3 Trace as subject · typeset diagram.** The stirred wave printed big as a textbook figure, its component partials hanging small beneath it in a band that sinks toward a stippled silt bed.
Strong: one bright subject, calm, clearly authored ("fig. 1", axes, stipple bed), and the sinking band says "sediment" without a legend. Weak at 1:1: the component band sits right at the 7 px spacing limit and reads a little like water hatching; layers caps at eight curves, so 16 to 64 barely change.

**v3 · P4 Stack in depth · orthographic elevation.** Octave bands as plates stepping back from the fundamental on a hatched ground line, upper plates lifted off dashed settled positions with dimension ticks.
Strong: the most literal picture of the DSP: stir visibly lifts the high bands more than the low ones, and "lifted vs settled" is the elevation's own hidden-line convention. Weak at 1:1: the plates read as shelves or a bar chart, the left and top of the body are empty, and settle and stir both lean on position.

**v4 · P5 Field and figure · survey plan.** A silt plume spreading in plan from an inlet over a contoured basin, with a deposit lens on the flow line and one chainage station per partial along the foot.
Strong: the strongest metaphor: grain moving the deposit upstream (coarse drops first) is something the player gets in one look, and the plan is calm. Weak at 1:1: the plume shows a picture of silt, not the partials, so stir doesn't show which partials lifted; the hairline contours are dim enough that they may disappear on the panel, and the station strip is close to a panel element.

**v5 · P11 Instrument stack (stretch) · patent section.** A glass settling column in section: strata up to a bright interface, a hinged stirring arm, suspended grains, reference numerals.
Strong: reads as one object at a glance, and the patent conventions (hatched walls, numerals, "fig. 1") make it look the most finished of the five. Weak: the stretch doesn't hold up. Nobody knows a settling column by sight, so P11's free recognition isn't there; it works as a P1 section drawing instead. Stir as a swinging arm is literal but doesn't show the high partials lifting.

## Ranking

1. v2: the strongest single subject, and the one that most clearly reads as both "a sound" and "sediment".
2. v3: the most faithful to what the DSP does (lift by band, settled vs lifted); needs a fuller composition.
3. v5: best-looking at 1:1, but it's a stretch for P11 and stir is shown as a metaphor.
4. v4: a good metaphor and calm field, but the furthest from the partials themselves.
5. v1: correct channels, but it reads as a dashboard.

The pick is the owner's.
