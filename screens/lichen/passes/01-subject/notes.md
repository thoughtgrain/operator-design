# lichen · subject pass 01 · notes

Sketches are rest frames at defaults (grow 35, decay 20, seed 1, maj), all from `sketch.py` and one colony model (12 × 6 isomorphic hex lattice, tick 14 of a fixed run: 14 living cells, 7 dead in the centre, 2 born this tick, one spore due). Judged at 1:1 (`vN/rest.png`). Every frame lints at 0 errors; the remaining warnings (v2 1, v5 4) are hairline (1–1.2 px) strokes on graticule/trig marks, sketch-level, to fix at frames. Muted state for each is in its `candidate.json` risks.

## v1 · P12 orrery · engineering schematic
What it is: each lattice row is an orbit round a root sun (tilted plan, chain centre lines, row numbers on the axis), cells are planets, the colony a cluster of filled planets with hollow dead ones, growth links along/across orbits, a dashed seed track outside.
Strong: calm and authored, the links show growth as motion along a cycle, and the bright born planet is the obvious focus. Weak at 1:1: the colony is a small cluster in a big ring system (most planets are empty ticks), six rings read close to a target, the sun is scenery, and wrapping rows into rings invents a neighbour the lattice doesn't have.

## v2 · P13 capture · oscilloscope trace
What it is: the lattice as a scope graticule of dots, the colony's growth front as one bright closed trace round everything it holds, the dead centre as a dim afterglow trace inside, born cells as bright spots.
Strong: the two traces are the rule itself (margin grows, centre dies), so grow and decay each own one trace and read without a legend; very clean at 1:1, lots of black. Weak at 1:1: the inner trace wanders into odd shapes when the dead cells are scattered, nested traces flirt with a contour map, and scale has only small labels to move.

## v3 · P14 loom · typeset diagram
What it is: one warp thread per scale degree in pitch order, four rows of ticks receding, slabs where a degree was alive, the front row this tick with bright caps on new growth, octave rules and a caption under it.
Strong: the only variant that shows history, and the front row reads instantly as "what just played" in pitch order; the typeset furniture looks authored. Weak at 1:1: it throws away the lattice (no colony, no dead centre), back rows are tiny flat tiles, and the right half of the warp is empty at rest.

## v4 · P5 field and figure · orthographic elevation
What it is: a running-bond wall in elevation (one stone per cell, which is exactly the hex lattice), the lichen as one lobed patch over it, hollow areolae where the centre died, bright growth at the margin, a tick strip at the foot.
Strong: the most literal and the strongest identity; one subject, one brightest outline, and the dead centre reads as cracked old crust with no explanation. Weak at 1:1: the grey body with a bright outline can read as a stain, the lobing is a wavy texture rather than drawn lobes, and the half-step joints that carry scale are too faint.

## v5 · P2 diagram + trace · survey plan (stretch)
What it is: left, the lattice surveyed as running-bond lots with the colony's boundary run as a traverse (stations at corners, dead lots marked, born lots solid); right, a levelled profile of the pitches played over the last bar.
Strong: the profile is the only place the melody itself is visible, and the plan's traverse is crisp at 1:1. Weak at 1:1: two halves compete, the profile is a note history list in another form, the trig triangle edges toward an icon. My read: it fits weakly; the profile is worth keeping as a note-response or page idea, not half the screen.

## Ranking (designer's, not a pick)

1. v4: the lichen itself on the lattice, one subject, every control on the patch or the wall.
2. v2: the rule drawn as two traces; cleanest at 1:1, weakest on scale.
3. v1: handsome and calm, but a stand-in metaphor with an idle sun and a target risk.
4. v3: strong history, but it loses the lattice and the colony's shape.
5. v5: doesn't really fit the player column; keep the profile idea.
