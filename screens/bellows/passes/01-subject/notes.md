# Bellows · subject pass 01 · notes

All five rest sketches at defaults (press 50, reed 40, leak 0, choir 0.00), lint 0 errors / 0 warnings, judged at 1:1 from `vN/rest.png`.

**v1 · P15 cell field · engineering schematic.** The wind chest as a plenum of 24 px cells, lit air rising from the floor, reed ports on the lid, a pressure dimension at the side.
Strong: hard edges, crisp at 1:1, and leak/choir have a natural home in the grain. Weak: at 1:1 it reads as a bar chart or level meter. The lit mass is about a third of the body, and Gate 0 argues against a texture pattern for a machine with a mechanism.

**v2 · P1 section · oscilloscope trace.** Bellows and reed block cut open on a graticule; pleats are a triangle trace, the tongue a bright stroke.
Strong: it's recognisably a bellows at a glance, each control owns a physical part, and the scope language gives a reason for the line-only drawing. Weak: the graticule and centre ticks compete with the drawing, the pleats outshine the reed that actually sings, and the reed block is small and right-crowded.

**v3 · P2 diagram + trace · typeset diagram.** Fig. 1: a weighted wedge reservoir feeding reeds a and b; fig. 2: the output wave under its sagging envelope.
Strong: it explains the machine (wind in, sound out). The trace is the brightest thing, and choir as envelope waists is honest to what you hear. Weak: two half-size pictures instead of one subject. The wedge reads thin and sparse at 1:1 next to a heavy trace, and the captions add text.

**v4 · P3 trace as subject · orthographic elevation.** The reed's wave standing on a datum like a facade, held under the bellows' wind line that sags and recovers, with mullions, level mark and dimension.
Strong: one big subject with lots of black. Press and the sag are drawn as the roofline, which is the clearest picture of "pressure sags and recovers" in the set. Weak: without the datum furniture it's an oscilloscope (P3's own pitfall). Three controls are path shapes, nearly the whole path budget, and the mullion spacing doesn't follow the piers.

**v5 · P12 orrery · survey plan (stretch).** A surveyed ring round the wind chest; reeds a and b are two stations whose angle apart is the beat.
Strong: choir is literally two bodies at slightly different rates, so held notes show the beat at the beat rate. Calm, lots of black, and a clear brightest station. Weak: it reads as a clock face at 1:1. Press, reed and leak are scenery bolted on, it's outside the category, and it fits only for choir.

## Ranking

1. v2: the mechanism itself, every control a part; fix the graticule weight and give the reed more of the frame.
2. v4: the strongest single image of the wind sagging and recovering; needs the elevation language pushed so it isn't a scope.
3. v3: the most explanatory, but split in two.
4. v5: a true metaphor for choir only.
5. v1: reads as a meter; wrong pattern for a machine with a mechanism.

The pick is the owner's.
