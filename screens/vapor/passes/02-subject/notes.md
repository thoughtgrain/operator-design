# Vapor · pass 02 subject · notes

All five sketched at defaults (vowel a, breath 25, throat 1.00, glide 60ms) by `sketch.py`, 3D parts modelled and projected through one camera per variant. Each lints at 0 errors with no `text-in-drawing`. Warnings are hairlines under 1.5 px, the 1.2 px dots of v1's dash-dot axis, v2's small breath dashes, and two sub-1/8 px point gaps on v5's ribbons. All were viewed at 1:1. Body black: v1 95 %, v2 96 %, v3 85 %, v4 91 %, v5 95 %. Uniqueness: `ready_for_pick`, the highest subject overlap is 0.09.

**v1 · exploded view · the speaking machine.** Wedge bellows, reed block, three telescoping throat sections, a flared horn and a lip iris, pulled apart along a dash-dot air axis, with the iris's aperture as the one bright line.
Strong: it looks like the hidden-line look the owner liked in pass 01, but now it's a machine anyone reads as "air in, voice out", and every knob is a part you could turn by hand. Weak: six parts is busy, the reed block and spring are small at 1:1, the iris is seen at an angle so the vowel is a foreshortened ellipse, and glide-as-spring is a metaphor.

**v2 · single-weight contour · the head, cut down the middle.** A singer's head in mid-sagittal section: nasal cavity, palate, the tongue's bright dome, the pharynx, the larynx low in the throat, breath dashes rising up the airway.
Strong: it reads as a head singing straight away, and the inside explains the voice. The tongue is the vowel, the larynx height is the throat, both literal. Weak: it's the phonetics-textbook figure and can tip into diagram. Single weight leaves hierarchy to grey, and at vowel a the glide hatch is only a thin band over the tongue.

**v3 · hatched mass · breath fogged on a cold pane.** A window, face on, with glazing bars. A sung breath has fogged the glass: a dense hatched core in the mouth's shape, a sparse halo round it, and drops running down to the bar.
Strong: hatching is exactly right for fog, and it's the calmest, most atmospheric frame of the five. The drops make glide a visible count. Weak: without the bars it's a texture. Throat as the patch's height on the glass is arbitrary. And it's the heaviest node bill (many hairlines).

**v4 · edge-lit silhouette · lips at the edge, breath on cold air.** The lower face at the left edge as a black silhouette with one bright rim, and a hatched plume of breath leaving the lips across the frame, a narrow oo stream first and then opening.
Strong: the most legible image in the pass: a person singing into cold air, readable in a second. The oo-then-open stream draws vapor's onset rule literally. Every control is on the body or the breath. Weak: a human profile risks illustration. The throat bump is in a corner. Vowel's lip change is near the 16 px shape floor. The plume is a large hatched area to redraw.

**v5 · detailed line art · a ring of vapour.** A breath ring modelled as a torus, its rolling flow as hidden-line hairlines under a bright silhouette, wisps peeling off, and the smaller hatched oo ring it opened from rising behind on a broken wake.
Strong: the machine's name drawn literally, with the 3D line-art detail the owner liked in pass 01. Plan shape-as-lips is a neat vowel mapping. Weak: at a glance it can read as a doughnut, tyre or lampshade. Vowel and throat both change the ring's size. Section lines bunch under 3 px at the sides.

## Ranking (a reviewer's ranking; the pick is the owner's)

1. v4: reads at a glance, and it's this machine's own rule (a breathy voice opening out of oo) as one image
2. v2: the mechanism itself, every control literal, very legible
3. v1: the line-art quality the owner liked, now as an object you can name; busy at 1:1
4. v5: the literal vapour, but the torus is at risk of reading as something else
5. v3: atmospheric and well hatched, but the weakest control mapping (throat)
