# Lathe · pass 01 · subject notes

All five sketches are rest frames at the defaults (cut 1.00, groove 0, wear 0, rumble 0), 480 × 220, stock header, lint 0 errors / 0 warnings, judged at 1:1 from `vN/rest.png` (rsvg-convert, black background). Generator: `sketch.py`. An effect page gets no note input, so in every variant the drawing only moves when a knob turns. At the defaults wear and rumble are 0, so their marks are absent from all five rest frames.

**v1 · P1 section drawing · typeset diagram.** The stylus mid-cut in a cross-section of the lacquer blank: a bright chisel, the V groove under it, three cut grooves behind, hatched lacquer on an aluminium core, swarf coiling off, labelled like a textbook figure.
Strong: one bright thing, and it's the part that makes the sound. cut deepening the V and flattening its floor is saturation drawn literally, and it's the cheapest (2 nodes at rest). Weak: a long, thin horizontal composition with a lot of hairlines, the hatching is near the 3 px floor, and wear's chips are small.

**v2 · P2 diagram + trace · orthographic elevation.** The lathe side-on (plinth, platter, cantilevered rail, cutter head) on the left; one turn of the played groove wall as a trace on the right.
Strong: it reads at once as "machine → what comes out", the trace is calm, and cut's flattened peaks are easy to read. Weak: the left half only carries groove, so three knobs act on the right and the column split fails. The boxes (plinth, rail, column) lean toward a panel, and all four mutable paths are spent.

**v3 · P3 trace as subject · survey plan.** A sector of the side from above: grey earlier turns as contours, the groove being cut drawn bright across it, chainage ticks (0+00…1+20), grid crosses and a scale bar.
Strong: the calmest and most authored-looking frame. It's unmistakably a record surface, and the labels give it the plan's voice. Weak: at groove 0 the label edge is off-frame, so the rest frame doesn't show groove at all. The bright groove costs three mutable paths, and it's close to P3's "just a wiggly line" pitfall.

**v4 · P4 stack in depth · patent section.** Five section slices from rim (10) to label (18) receding diagonally, hatched faces, waves shortening toward the label, the front slice lit.
Strong: the patent language is consistent (section lining, numerals, fig. 2), and "inner grooves carry shorter waves" is real physics. groove as the stack's scan position is P4 used exactly. Weak: five hatched faces are a lot of texture for 1:1, groove steps between slices and must ease, and the player has to know the physics for it to explain itself.

**v5 · P11 instrument stack (stretch) · hidden-line isometric.** The lathe assembled: plinth, platter and lacquer, the rail behind, carriage and arm, the bright cutter head over the disc.
Strong: instantly recognisable as a turntable-like machine, crisp hidden-line drawing, cheap. Weak: P11 doesn't really fit, because no note selects a part and an effect gets no notes. It's a portrait of the machine, not of the sound. The swarf coil that carries cut is barely visible at rest, and rumble's in-plane turn of the disc outline is a fake tilt.

## Ranking (designer's view; the pick is the owner's)

1. v1: the subject is the cut itself, every control moves part of it physically, and it's the cheapest.
2. v3: the best-looking frame and clearly a record, but groove is invisible at the default, and cut takes three paths.
3. v4: the strongest single drawing language, with an honest mapping; heavy texture, and the physics is abstract.
4. v2: clear, but it splits into two halves that don't match the hands, and it's boxy.
5. v5: the stretch doesn't fit; it draws the machine, not what the machine does to the sound.
