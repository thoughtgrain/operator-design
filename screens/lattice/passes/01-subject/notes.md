# lattice · subject pass 01 · notes

Sketches are rest frames at defaults (size 8, tension 40, damp 30, pickup 60), all from `sketch.py` and one modal model of the plate. Judged at 1:1 (`vN/rest.png`). Every frame lints at 0 errors; remaining warnings are hairline (1 px) guides, sub-3 px dashes/ticks in v1/v4/v5 and one near-reversal at a sharp peak of v3's curve, all sketch-level and to fix at frames.

## v1 · P15 cell field · patent section
What it is: the plate face on as an 8 × 8 field of junction cells lit by displacement, in a hatched clamp frame, with numerals 10–16 and leader lines.
Strong: the mesh really is a grid, so the field is literal, and the hatched frame says "held at the edges" in one look. Weak at 1:1: the lit pattern is one blob in a small box (112 px), the frame and numerals outweigh it, and words beside numerals double the key.

## v2 · P1 section drawing · hidden-line isometric
What it is: the sheet in isometric, mesh lines bent into the standing wave, bolted frame, striker rod, stylus pickup and a hanging felt damper.
Strong: the most immediately legible "metal plate struck here, heard there"; every control has a real physical part, and the bright plate edge is a clear single brightest element. Weak at 1:1: the bent mesh is the expensive part (many lines, past the 4-path limit), damper and striker head are small and edge toward icon, leader lines cross the drawing.

## v3 · P2 diagram + trace · star atlas
What it is: the plate as a star chart (junctions as stars sized by how hard they ring, strike-to-pickup constellation line) beside the light curve the pickup records.
Strong: the right side is the only variant that shows what you hear over time (damp reads instantly), and the atlas labelling looks authored. Weak at 1:1: two pictures compete; the curve is the brightest and becomes the subject; tension as a sagging constellation line is a metaphor, not physics.

## v4 · P3 trace as subject · engineering schematic
What it is: the standing wave cut along the strike→pickup line, drawn as a beam between fixed supports with a force arrow, a probe to "out" and a dimensioned span.
Strong: calmest and cleanest at 1:1, lots of black, one bright trace, schematic conventions (supports, chain line, dimension) feel native. Weak at 1:1: it throws away the plate's second dimension, the machine's whole point; at rest the trace is nearly flat and the screen can read as "a string", not a plate.

## v5 · P12 orrery · oscilloscope trace (stretch)
What it is: one orbit per mode round the strike point, radius from frequency, a phosphor trail behind each body as long as that mode rings, body size from its strength at the pickup.
Strong: phosphor trail as ring time is a genuinely good mapping, and modes really are independent rotators. Weak at 1:1: concentric rings read as a target, the graticule adds texture without meaning, nothing says "plate", and the centre does nothing. My read: it doesn't fit this category.

## Ranking (designer's, not a pick)

1. v2 — the object itself, every control on a physical part.
2. v1 — literal to the mesh, strongest identity, needs a bigger field.
3. v3 — best at showing damp, but two subjects.
4. v4 — beautiful and calm, but a string, not a plate.
5. v5 — doesn't fit; keep the trail idea.
