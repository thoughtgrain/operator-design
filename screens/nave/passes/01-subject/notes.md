# Nave · pass 01 (subject) · notes

All five drawn by `sketch.py` at the defaults (nave 2.2 s, stone 50, choir 60, doors 20 ms), linted at 0 errors, judged at 1:1 from `rest.png`, and checked at all-0 and all-100 (probes not kept). Uniqueness: `ready_for_pick`; every pair differs in pattern and language, subject word overlap ≤ 0.11. Remaining lint warnings are size notes only: one 2.8 px dash end (v1), stars under 3 px on the back charts (v4), and the 2 px dither cells P9 is built from (v5).

**v1 · P1 Section drawing · survey plan.** The church in plan, cut at sill height: bright poché walls, two arcade rows with bay numbers, choir stalls and screen, a round apse, a porch with door swings, a centre line, a scale bar, and one ray from the door bouncing wall to wall.
Strong: one calm, authored subject that is the nave itself, and every control moves a part of the building (apse east, screen west, porch west, ray bounces). Weak at 1:1: three controls are position, the ray's zigzag is the busiest thing on screen while carrying the score-3 control, and the stall hairlines (choir) are faint.

**v2 · P2 Diagram + trace · patent section.** Fig. 1 a longitudinal section (hatched cuts, pointed arcade, vault, half-dome apse, reference numerals, arrows from the door); fig. 2 the impulse response with a direct impulse, a door gap and a treble envelope.
Strong: fig. 2 is the most honest picture of the DSP (pre-delay gap, decay, treble falling faster), and the patent conventions look authored. Weak at 1:1: two figures read as a document; the knobs don't split by column (1+3 left, 2+4 right); the brightest thing is the impulse, not the nave.

**v3 · P3 Trace as subject · hidden-line isometric.** A waterfall of twelve hidden-line wedges, bass in front to treble at the back, behind a bright direct-sound bar.
Strong: the boldest single shape, with stone shown exactly as the DSP does it (the back slices shortening) and choir as echo notches. Weak at 1:1: the back third is close to hatching, three controls are shape on one surface, twelve outlines don't fit four mutable paths, and nothing says "nave".

**v4 · P4 Stack in depth · star atlas.** Five charts with graduated edges receding up-right, stars by magnitude, the front chart bright and labelled "I first", a source star "door" with a dashed trail.
Strong: calm, lots of black, and the four channels are cleanly separate (spacing, star scale, star count, trail length); the deep stack at 12 s is a good picture of a long tail. Weak at 1:1: it reads as stacked cards before it reads as time, back-chart stars are tiny, and the atlas is a borrowed language with no stone in it.

**v5 · P9 One-bit ink (stretch) · engineering schematic.** A one-bit church in side elevation (tower, lancet nave, apse) with dithered walls, a decay squiggle over the roof, ink squares for choir and a dimension line for the doors.
Strong: recognised in a glance, crisp at 1:1, nave's lengthening is unmistakable. Weak: the stretch only partly holds. A church is known by sight and an effect page keeps one subject, but nothing on the figure performs the sound, so stone, choir and doors land on ornament glyphs (choir's squares mean nothing to a player), dither can't change on the device, and one-bit print plus schematic callouts is two languages.

## Ranking

1. v1: the nave as the subject, in one language, every control moving the building.
2. v2: the truest to the sound; needs one figure to lead, or the columns realigned.
3. v4: clean channels and calm, but the metaphor has to be explained.
4. v3: the strongest shape, blocked by the four-path budget and no sense of place.
5. v5: the most legible picture, the weakest mapping; it doesn't really fit.

The pick is the owner's.
