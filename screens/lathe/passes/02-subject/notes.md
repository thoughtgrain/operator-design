# Lathe · pass 02 · subject notes

All five are rest frames at the defaults (cut 1.00, groove 0, wear 0, rumble 0): 480 × 220, stock header, no text below it, grey only. Each lints with 0 errors and 0 `text-in-drawing`. The only warnings are legibility notes on 1 px hairline outlines and a few tiny ribbon end pieces. I judged each from `vN/rest.png` at 1:1 (rsvg-convert, black background). The body is 89–93 % black in every frame, and #CDCDCD is the one brightest element. The generator is `sketch.py`, and v3, v4 and v5 are each modelled in 3D and projected through one camera. An effect page gets no note input, so in every variant the drawing moves only when a knob turns. Wear and rumble are 0 at the defaults, so their marks don't appear in these frames.

**v1 · the lathe, literally · single-weight contour.** A turning lathe in profile, with the work between the chuck and the tailstock centre. Raw stock sits on the left of the tool and the turned profile on the right, and that profile is the cut signal (the owner's requested profile view).
At 1:1 it reads instantly and calmly, the bright profile is unmistakably the subject, and cut flattening its crests is saturation you can see. Weak points: it's a metal lathe rather than a record lathe, the tool, post and chip are small and a bit fussy, and rumble's runout turn is under the rotation floor.

**v2 · the cutter head · hatched mass.** The record lathe's head riding its lead screw, side-on. Carriage, head and slab are hatched masses, and the bright stylus hangs from its pivot into the lacquer.
At 1:1 the mechanism is clear, the stylus is the one bright body, and groove as carriage travel along the screw is very legible (~190 px). Weak points: there's a lot of 45° texture that may shimmer on the panel when the head moves, the right third is empty at the default, and cut's stylus swing is small at rest.

**v3 · the lacquer, close · edge-lit silhouette.** Grooves recede from the eye at grazing light, with only their lips lit. The newest groove is bright, and the near cut face is a notched silhouette.
At 1:1 it's the most striking and the most "material", the rim bevel at the right shows groove 0, and it's unlike any other screen. Weak points: converging lines lean toward a retro perspective grid, the upper-left lips get dense, and moving the whole field for groove is expensive.

**v4 · playback scene · detailed line art.** The cut disc on a platter, seen close and low. The grooves are hairline rings, the tonearm is swung in, and one bright arc marks the groove being read.
At 1:1 it reads as a record at once, and the tonearm swing for groove is a natural rotation. Weak points: it shows the playback deck, not the lathe, the arm and headshell are small (~10 px), the stroked 1 px rings draw lint warnings, and rumble moving the disc is a large redraw.

**v5 · the rule as an object · hidden-line 3D.** A specimen block of lacquer on aluminium with one modulated groove cut along it. The end face is sectioned and hatched, and the lit far lip is the subject.
At 1:1 it's crisp and well-proportioned, and every knob reads as a physical change to the object (deeper, shorter, chipped, heaved). Weak points: cut and groove both re-key the groove's paths (across vs along), which bends the one-channel rule, and the neighbouring turns' lips come close to the main lips in places.

## Ranking (designer's view; the pick is the owner's)

1. v1: the owner's profile view, the calmest frame, and the waveform-as-turned-profile idea only fits this machine.
2. v5: the strongest drawing, and the most direct "what each knob does to a thing", but its channels need proving.
3. v2: the honest mechanism, very legible, but heavy texture.
4. v3: the most original look, but it risks reading as a perspective grid and is costly to move.
5. v4: a good picture of playback, but the wrong half of the machine and too small at 1:1.
