# Pendula · pass 01 (subject) · notes

All five rest frames come from `sketch.py`, and all lint with 0 errors. They're rendered to `vN/rest.png` with `rsvg-convert -w 480 -h 220 -b black` and judged at 1:1. Rest is the moment before release: the transport is stopped, every bob is held at the same release point and nothing sounds. The drift only appears in playing frames, which aren't drawn yet.

**v1 · P13 Capture · typeset diagram.** A textbook figure: every pendulum hangs from one pivot on nested swing arcs, the plumb line is the gate, θ is marked at the pivot, and a pitch-class polygon sits in the corner for scale.
Strong at 1:1: it's the calmest and most legible of the five, the gate maps the machine's rule exactly, and once playing, the bobs fan from one ray into a spiral and back. Weak: one pivot is a diagram, not the apparatus. At 16 pendulums the inner bobs crowd to about 7 px apart. Scale lives in a corner, as it does in meikyu.

**v2 · P14 Loom · orthographic elevation.** A front elevation of the row. The strings carry on down as warp threads into a woven cloth where each crossing of the last four beats is a slab, and the threads are spaced by the scale's intervals.
Strong at 1:1: it's the only one where the drift, the thing that makes a pendulum wave a pendulum wave, is visible as history (diagonals across the rows), and scale gets a real part of the drawing instead of a corner. Weak: seen face on, the swing goes towards the viewer, so it only reads as the bobs lifting (about 26 px at most). Four rows is short history, and slabs at 16 threads are expensive.

**v3 · P5 Field and figure · survey plan.** Seen from overhead: the row lies along a dashed datum on a survey grid, a release board holds every bob on one line, and the strip along the bottom is a two-octave pitch ruler.
Strong at 1:1: the release board is an honest, physical rest state, and the strip shows the actual notes. Weak: it's the most chart-like of the five. From above, length is invisible, so it borrows the lane span, and swing falls back on a symbol-size convention. The grid adds texture without adding meaning.

**v4 · P12 Orrery · patent section.** A patent sheet of conical pendulums on one spindle: orbits stacked into a cone, a hatched hub, a cutting plane A–A as the gate, a base dial for scale and reference numerals.
Strong at 1:1: it's the most handsome drawing and the most "authored". It's properly modelled in 3D, and when playing the bobs would spread round their orbits like planets. Weak: a conical pendulum is a different machine and asks the player to accept that its shadow is the swing. One A label is lost among the orbits, the hub hatching is under 3 px, and the base dial is a ring of ticks, which reads as a weak control.

**v5 · P6 Figures (stretch) · hidden-line isometric.** A swing set with a block rider on every seat, chains of slowly different lengths, and a tuned bar on a post under each swing for the rider's feet to strike.
Strong at 1:1: you recognise a row of swings instantly, and the bar lengths make scale physical. Weak: at 8 riders the figures are about 15 px tall and read as a row of chairs, and 16 would be a crowd. Swinging riders need pre-drawn poses because a node can't tilt out of the screen's plane. It doesn't earn the exception: the pendulum is already the performer, so a rider adds a character without adding meaning.

## Ranking

1. **v1**: the clearest picture of the rule (cross the gate, play), and it reads best at 1:1.
2. **v2**: the only one that shows the drift as history; scale as thread spacing is the best scale mapping in the pass.
3. **v4**: the strongest drawing, but it changes the machine (conical pendulums) to fit the pattern.
4. **v3**: honest rest state, but chart-like, and two of its controls lean on conventions.
5. **v5**: the stretch; it doesn't fit, as said in its risks.

The pick is the owner's.
