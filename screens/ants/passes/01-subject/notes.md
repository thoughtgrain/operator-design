# Ants · pass 01 · subject notes

All five sketches are rest frames at the defaults (ants 4, scent 50, fade 30, scale pent), 480 × 220, stock header, lint 0 errors / 0 warnings, judged at 1:1 from `vN/rest.png` (rsvg-convert, black background). Generator: `sketch.py`. All five draw the same colony state (two pentatonic octaves, one reinforced phrase 1 3 5 1' 6 3 2 1, two fading older trails, four ants), so the differences are the subject, not the data. Uniqueness: `design_pass_submit` → `ready_for_pick`, all ten pairs differ in pattern and language, subject overlap ≤ 0.12.

**v1 · P12 orrery · typeset diagram.** The nest as a dark sphere, two octave orbits round it, degrees as planets at their pitch-class angles, the trail as bright links (spiral links between octaves), degree numerals and a figure caption.
Strong: reads as an orrery at once, the lattice's pitch classes are real geometry (spokes), scale has an obvious channel (planets per orbit). Weak at 1:1: the phrase reuses arcs, so the trail doubles into a thick ring on the left; ants are beads on a bright line and barely separate from it; the nest does nothing.

**v2 · P13 capture · orthographic elevation.** An elevation across the lattice: each walked degree has built a flat-topped mound of scent, a dashed waterline (fade) drowns the low ones to grey, ants stand on the dry crests, earth ticks under the ground line.
Strong: the capture rule is real (fade decides what survives, and the waterline is the clearest single picture of that), every control moves something you can point at, one bright profile is the subject. Weak at 1:1: a row of peaks over a band reads a little like a waveform or meter; the phrase's order is lost, so it shows which degrees, not which tune.

**v3 · P14 loom · survey plan.** Eight step stations across, four passes plotted as traverses (height = degree), the newest bright with square stations at the front, older ones finer and greyer behind, a degree ruler at the left margin.
Strong: the only one that shows "trails strengthen into repeated phrases" directly (rows that match are a phrase that has set); calm, crisp, cheap (8 bound nodes). Weak at 1:1: it's the nearest to a line chart of the five; depth is only row position and grey; the ruler that carries scale is small.

**v4 · P5 field and figure · patent section.** A skewed lattice field (step and third neighbours) cut at the front with a hatched section, the trail as one bright loop through seven nodes, dashed ghost loops, reference numerals 10–16, FIG. 2, a step strip below.
Strong: the lattice is the most honest model of the machine (a 2D degree lattice, not a line), one figure is clearly the subject, patent numerals look authored. Weak at 1:1: the skewed field fans into perspective noise, the loop reads as a ring lying on a grid rather than a walked path, and the section is costume: nothing in it moves.

**v5 · P11 instrument stack (stretch) · hidden-line isometric.** A formicarium on a plinth, a chamber per degree, the octave galleries winding back and forth up the slab, the trail as the bright tunnel, collapsing old tunnels dashed.
Strong: instantly recognisable, hidden-line slab is crisp and sits in the same family as theseus. Weak at 1:1: the octave-one gallery's tunnels bunch along the isometric diagonal into a tangle, the slab is a third of the screen of frame that never changes, and the stretch case is weak: it recognises the container, not the rule.

## Ranking (designer's view; the pick is the owner's)

1. v2: the strongest single picture of the rule, and fade (the hardest control to show) gets the clearest channel; its phrase-order gap is the thing to solve.
2. v3: tells the phrase story best and is the cleanest at 1:1; needs to look less like a chart.
3. v1: a sound P12, but the trail doubling and the idle nest need a redraw.
4. v5: recognisable and well drawn, but a stretch that doesn't earn its exception.
5. v4: the best lattice model in the weakest picture.
