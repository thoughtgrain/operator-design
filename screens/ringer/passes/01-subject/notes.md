# Ringer · subject pass 01 · notes

Five sketches, one script (`sketch.py`), all at defaults (6 bells, plain bob, 2 a beat, stroke 0), rows from real place notation. Every `rest.svg` lints at 0 errors (warnings are hairlines at 1.0–1.2 px, which `craft.md` allows, and a few short hatch/tick parts). Judged at 1:1 from `vN/rest.png`.

- **v1 · P12 orrery · patent section.** Each bell on its own orbit, its angle its place; a hunting bell travels its orbit, which is literally what the treble does.
  Strong: one calm object, a true cycle, 3D model with one camera. Weak at 1:1: the bells are 15–30 px and read close to bell glyphs; the section cut barely shows; the arbor does nothing.
- **v2 · P13 capture · hidden-line isometric.** The bells in their frame, each turned to how far it has swung, because a bell's place in the change is where it is in its swing.
  Strong: the only candidate where the picture is the rule itself, and mute has an honest state (the ring stood at the balance). Weak at 1:1: busy; the row has to be read from bell sizes, the ropes/sallies are faint, and swing out of the screen plane needs pre-drawn variants.
- **v3 · P14 loom · star atlas.** The method as a sky chart: changes are parallels, places meridians, the treble's path the constellation.
  Strong: the most legible at 1:1, the method's shape is visible at once, and the atlas furniture makes it look authored. Weak: it is the blue line, the ringer's method diagram, close to a grid of data; sliding every star back each change blows the redraw budget.
- **v4 · P5 field and figure · engineering schematic.** One bell in elevation turned to the angle it's rung to, over a chamber floor, the whole pull in the strip.
  Strong: one bold, bright figure, cheap (in-plane rotation). Weak: the figure only carries pace; the method lives in a 16 px strip of ticks, so the subject fights the machine and reads as a single bell.
- **v5 · P4 stack in depth · oscilloscope trace (stretch).** Each change as its pitch staircase on a graticule, earlier changes stacked back as sweeps.
  Strong: clean, legible, mute is a flat trace. Weak: it draws the output, not the rule; it's P14's job done less well, and P3's oscilloscope pitfall. Honestly it doesn't fit the category.

## Ranking (the designer's; the pick is the owner's)

1. **v2**: the P13 idea at full strength. The picture is the rule, so every control has a physical twin. Needs the most work and the heaviest budget.
2. **v3**: the clearest at 1:1 and the most authored-looking; its risk is redraw and drifting into a data grid.
3. **v1**: a good-looking cycle, but the bells are glyph-sized and the sun is idle.
4. **v4**: a strong figure that carries the wrong thing.
5. **v5**: doesn't fit the category; kept as the honest stretch.
